# Phase 12 roadmap (October 2026)

An eleventh gap analysis, made as Phase 11 drew to a close, three days after the Phase 11 analysis of 26 October. It compares Montage with Premiere 26.5.2 (no 26.6 or 27.0 was found; Premiere 26.0 came in January 2026 rather than at Adobe MAX, so the next major release may not come at MAX either) and After Effects 26.5 with its beta (Adobe's beta page, updated 7 October: the AI Assistant, which takes user-provided skills from beta build 27.1; a Contour Paint effect; animated precomps, solids, images and videos used as materials on 3D objects in the Advanced 3D renderer; Substance materials on text and shapes). It also covers DaVinci Resolve 21.1.2 (Newsshooter's report is dated 26 October: loudness meters in the edit mixer, OGraf and Lottie on Linux, faster IntelliSearch, fixes to Film Look Creator and Music Remixer; no 21.2 has been announced), Final Cut Pro 12.4 (29 September), and the Final Cut Pro update Apple announced on 27 October for Apple Creator Studio subscribers, due in November. That update brings MotionVFX content, a Content Hub of stock media, image generation, round trips to Pixelmator Pro, more keyframe easing, clips disconnected from the primary storyline and 23 more transcription languages. Apple gave no version number, so whether it is the "Final Cut Pro 13" leaked on 1 October was not settled. Media Composer 2026.8 is the latest version found (no 2026.10). The analysis also looks at Baselight 8 as Digital Production described it on 11 September (due in 2027), Nuke 17.1 (no 17.2 was found), Pro Tools 2026.4.1 (9 July; no 2026.10 was found), Nuendo 15, and iZotope RX 12 (28 April 2026), which is new to these analyses: Scene Rebalance, Trim Silence, Stems View, and a rebuilt De-bleed and Breath Control. For Kdenlive it looks at 26.08.2 (8 October) and the call for testing for 26.12 (16 October: relative project paths and mixed styles in the titler). For Shotcut it looks at 26.9.27 (28 September); a 26.10.3 AppImage on GitHub (3 October) has no announcement, so its status is unclear. Among open tools it looks at FFmpeg 9.0.2 (18 September). FFmpeg 9.1 has been branched but no release was found; it brings a Vulkan APV encoder, `vf_fruc_vulkan` frame interpolation, a rewritten AAC encoder, ASTC images, a Panasonic RW2 decoder, DVD-Audio LPCM and xHE-AAC encoding through libfdk-xhe-aac. It also looks at OpenColorIO 2.6.0 (no 2.6.1 found), OpenTimelineIO 0.18.1 (the documentation shows 0.19.0.dev1, but no release was found), OpenFX 1.5.1 (no 1.6 found) and AV2. For AV2, AVM 1.0 came out on 28 May. VideoLAN announced its dav2d decoder on 31 May under a BSD-style licence; it was then a feature-complete AVM v15 decoder still being optimised. FFmpeg still has no AV2 support (Forasoft, 16 October). Four findings change what is reachable:
- **The SMPTE library is free.** SMPTE made its whole standards library free to everyone on 17 June 2026: standards, recommended practices, engineering guidelines and RDDs. ST 2127-1, whose cost kept S-ADM out of Phase 11 #13, and the ST 2110 suite can now be read.
- **Photon checks S-ADM.** Netflix's Photon checks ST 2067-203 (frame-based S-ADM) from version 4.10.0. ST 2067-204 (ADM audio) is checked in a fork whose pull request upstream (#421) is still open; review fixes were merged into the fork on 25 October 2026.
- **ASC FDL.** The ASC Framing Decision List specification is at v2.0.1, with an Apache-2.0 reference library (`libfdl_core`, C++ with a C interface).
- **Netflix's subtitle timing rules.** Netflix's Timed Text Style Guide gives its shot-change rules in half-seconds rather than the old 12 frames.

Every candidate was checked against `README.md`, the source in `src/` and the `PROGRESS.md` checklists with their "Not done" and "Not yet" notes. These are already there and are not proposed again.
- **From Phase 11:** titles styled letter by letter, film-mix automation, mesh and pin warp, project merging, IMF App #5, BT.2446-1, cinema loudness, shot groups, light rays and aperture glare, USD, Gaussian splats, the node graph, IAB, remote grading and translations.
- **From earlier phases:** everything the Phase 11 roadmap listed as already there.
- **Brought by recent releases, already in Montage:**
  - Groups made by machine learning (Baselight 8; Phase 11 #8).
  - A LUT rebuilt as editable grades (Chromogen ML; Phase 10).
  - Remote sessions (Baselight 8; Phase 11 #14).
  - Scene Rebalance (RX 12; Montage's dialogue, music and effects split).
  - De-bleed and Breath Control (RX 12; mic bleed removal and Phase 9).
  - Round trips to an image editor (Final Cut Pro; Edit Original, and changed media reloads).
  - Bézier keyframe easing.
  - OGraf and multicam switching by speaker (Resolve 21.1.2).
  - Mixed styles in one title (Kdenlive 26.12; Phase 11 #1).
  - An assistant that edits through tools (After Effects beta; the MCP server and Assistant panel).

Dolby Vision, per-bin locking, direct publishing and generated media stay out.

Ranking is value to a professional editor, colourist, sound editor or finishing artist × feasibility in C++/Qt/FFmpeg with optional local ONNX and GGUF models. Items 1–12 can be tested completely in CI and come first; 13–15 each have a part that cannot be checked here, said in the row. Each row ends with what is out of reach. Nothing below needs a paid specification, an account, a cloud API or an API key. No new model is proposed, and no closed SDK is used. Every dependency is GPL-3.0-compatible unless **flagged**. The licences below were read from each project's repository page on 29 October:
- the ASC's `fdl` reference implementation (Apache-2.0)
- libdatachannel (MPL-2.0 since 0.18; earlier versions were LGPL-2.1-or-later)
- the Open Visual Cloud Media Transport Library (BSD-3-Clause)
- Sony's `nmos-cpp` (Apache-2.0)
- AMWA's `nmos-testing` (Apache-2.0)
- the EBU's `libadm` (Apache-2.0; it does not read S-ADM, so #6 writes and reads S-ADM itself)

Everything can be built and tested in the Linux cloud container (and on macOS and Windows in the Package workflow) except where a row says otherwise. Sizes: S ≤ 2, M 2–6, L 6–12 person-weeks.

| 1 | Elastic audio: warp markers inside a clip to move a word, a hit or a beat without cutting, and a pitch curve to change the intonation of a line, with formants kept | Pro Tools (Elastic Audio), Nuendo (Free Warp, and VariAudio for pitch, timing and formants of monophonic recordings), Resolve's Fairlight (Elastic Wave retiming with timing points), iZotope RX 11 and 12 Advanced (Dialogue Contour, with Formant and Variation controls from RX 11) | M | **The gap.** Montage stretches whole clips with WSOLA along a time map (`audio/TimeStretch`: speed, ramps, keep pitch), lines ADR up to a guide (dialogue alignment) and hosts ARA editors. It cannot pin and move points inside a clip, and it cannot change the pitch of speech itself. **Timing.** Warp markers on audio clips (Alt-drag in the clip, or added at detected onsets). Each marker ties a source sample to a sequence time, and the existing WSOLA plays the piecewise-linear map between markers. Onsets come from spectral flux, for snapping. A group or linked clips (several microphones on one scene) share one map, so the timing changes stay phase-coherent. There are three modes: Speech, General and Varispeed (the pitch follows the speed). **Pitch.** pYIN (Mauch and Dixon, 2014) tracks the fundamental of a clip's voiced stretches, drawn as a curve over the waveform. A stretch can be shifted, flattened, have its inflection scaled (an ending made to fall, as Dialogue Contour does), or be corrected towards a scale for singing. The voice is resynthesised by TD-PSOLA at pitch marks (Moulines and Charpentier, 1990), with a separate formant shift. Both stay editable on the clip, render in playback through the audio cache, and are one undo step per change. Exports and AAF carry the rendered sound with handles. MCP `montage_warp_audio` and `montage_pitch_contour`. **Test:** a 440 Hz tone with a marker moved from 1.0 to 1.2 s makes the first stretch 1.2 times as long, still at 440 Hz within 1 cent. Clicks at known times are found within 1 ms. Two microphone channels warped as a group keep their cross-correlation lag within one sample. A synthetic vowel gliding from 120 to 100 Hz is tracked within 5 cents. A two-semitone shift lands within 5 cents while the LPC formant peaks move less than 3 %. With no markers or pitch edits the sound is sample-identical to today's. Export equals playback. **Out of reach:** whether edited speech sounds natural needs listening; polyphonic material (harmonies, chords) is not pitch-edited |

| 8 | Tape and archive sound repair: wow and flutter taken out, azimuth error between channels corrected, and silence stripped from clips | iZotope RX 11 and 12 Advanced (Wow & Flutter, Azimuth with Adaptive mode, and RX 12's Trim Silence, 28 April 2026), Pro Tools (Strip Silence) | S–M | In-house, in `audio/AudioRepair`. **Wow and flutter.** The speed of the transfer is measured over time from the stable partials in the sound: spectral peaks followed from frame to frame, weighted by harmonic agreement. A pilot or hum tone can be named when the tape has one. The deviation is split into wow (below about 4 Hz) and flutter (above), each with its own amount. The sound is then resampled along the inverse time map with a windowed sinc. Unlike WSOLA this is true variable-rate resampling, so pitch and speed are put back together. **Azimuth.** The delay between the channels is measured with GCC-PHAT, fixed for the clip or adaptive over time, and corrected with fractional-delay filters, with a polarity check. **Strip Silence.** A threshold, a shortest silence, and pads before and after split the selected clips, removing what lies under the threshold. MCP `montage_add_effect` gains `wow_flutter` and `azimuth`, and there is a new `montage_strip_silence`. **Test:** music with a 1 kHz tone, modulated by 0.8 Hz wow of ±0.3 % and 12 Hz flutter of ±0.05 %, comes back with less than 0.02 % deviation. A right channel delayed by 0.37 samples is measured within 0.02 samples and corrected so that L − R of a band-limited mono source nulls below −60 dB. Strip Silence on tone bursts leaves regions at the exact samples, with the pads. Undo restores everything. **Out of reach:** wow in material with no stable partials (speech alone) is measured less well, and real archive transfers with known speed errors are not available here |

The primaries are shown moving on the chromaticity scope. Settings copy to other shots, and the camera matrix under LED light can be fitted from a picked chart. **Colour Correlation.** Saturation changed by moving the channels' correlation about their luminance-weighted mean, inside boundary primaries (Rec.709, P3 or custom).
