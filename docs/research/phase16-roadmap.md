# Phase 16 roadmap (November 2026)

A fifteenth gap analysis, made on 15 November 2026 as Phase 15 was finished on draft PR #65 (head a8df2ce; main is 883d5d2 of 12 November, the merge of PR #64), six days after the Phase 15 analysis of 9 November. **Adobe MAX brought the week's two large releases, Premiere 27.0 and After Effects 27.0 (both 10 November). Also new since 9 November: OpenEXR 3.5.4 (its page says 12 November, its change log 11 November), libjxl 0.13.0 (11 November), ONNX Runtime 1.32.0 (13 November) and Avid's Ready to Play extension for Media Composer (10 November; it runs on Google's cloud). Five older releases were not in the Phase 15 analysis: the Nuke 18.0 open beta (27 October), SVT-AV1 4.3.0 (2 November), OBS Studio 33.0 (23 October, with 33.0.1 on 29 October), OpenUSD 26.11.1 (21 October) and OpenEXR 3.4.18 and 3.3.16 (8 November).** For the rest, nothing new was found. DaVinci Resolve 21.2 is still the newest (Newsshooter's article was updated on 9 November and names no later version). Final Cut Pro 12.4 is still at the top of Apple's release notes; Final Cut Pro 13 was announced in October for November and had not shipped by FCP Cafe's entry of 15 November. A search found no point release of Logic Pro 13.0 or Pro Tools 2026.11, and no Media Composer newer than the 2026.8 of the Phase 15 analysis. Nuke 17.1 is the newest final release, with 18.0 and 17.2 in beta. Baselight is at v7, with version 8 expected in 2027. Kdenlive is at 26.08.3; the 26.12 beta (26.11.80) was due on 13 November and Arch Linux tagged it that day, but no list of its changes was found. Shotcut's newest is still the v26.11 beta of 31 October, and Olive has nothing new. OpenTimelineIO 0.18.1 and OpenColorIO 2.6.0 are still the newest on their releases pages. FFmpeg's download page still has 9.0.2 at the top, and 9.1 is not listed. Qt 6.12.0, a long-term release, came out on 30 September (not noted before); 6.12.1 is planned for 17 November. libvmaf v3.2.1 is still the newest and no release has an HDR model. SVT-JPEG-XS v0.10.0 (30 October) is the newest and is what Montage pins. For AV2, AVM's GitHub page lists no releases, and VideoLAN's repository refused this analysis again. Among standards, the second edition of HLS (draft 22) moved through the RFC Editor's queue on 9 and 12 November with no new revision, and SMPTE's list has nothing newer than ST 2094-2 of 22 October. A search found only working drafts at the W3C in November, and no new IS-04 or IS-05 from AMWA. Seven findings matter here:
- **Premiere 27.0 (10 November).**
  - Colour: a Colour mode beside Edit, and masks made by hue, saturation and luminance.
  - Sound: the Speed/Duration dialog gains stretch modes and pitch, Auto Ducking's keyframes follow the clips as they are added, trimmed or muted, waveforms are drawn on the timeline while recording, and iXML metadata is shown on clips. Separate Crosstalk splits two people talking at once; it runs in Adobe's cloud and is charged by the second.
  - Effects: eight new ones (Bevel Alpha, Bevel & Emboss, Posterize, Radial Blur and Zoom Blur are named), and an Effects panel with animated thumbnails and a preview on the user's own footage.
  - Platforms: a native build for Windows on ARM, and no more Intel Macs.
  - Generated images, music, sound effects and soundscapes, Generative Extend across transitions and an AI Assistant panel, all in Adobe's cloud.
- **After Effects 27.0 (10 November).** Layers and whole compositions as the materials of 3D objects, materials on each face of 3D text and shapes (Substance files), a Contour Paint effect, a De-esser, a compression setting for PNG sequences, Illustrator text imported as editable text, auto-save that also offers the newest save after a crash, and an AI Assistant.
- **The Nuke 18.0 open beta (27 October).** Gaussian splats can be thinned in the viewport, drawn only inside the camera's view or as points, and picked out by position, scale, opacity, colour and distance to their neighbours (a FieldThreshold node). The selection tools work on splats. Nuke reads H.265 directly, and Nuke Studio finds the cuts in a reference movie. A node runs Griptape's AI workflows (a separate engine).
- **SVT-AV1 4.3.0 (2 November).** Its change log adds "timed film grain tables" (`--fgs-table`) and film grain parameters for each picture through a new API. Montage measures a shot's grain but cannot send it beside the picture (#6).
- **`KHR_gaussian_splatting` is ratified.** Khronos's page for the glTF extension reads "Complete, Ratified by the Khronos Group". A search dates this to 3 September, from one third party. Montage reads splats from PLY and SPZ files only (#8).
- **Security releases of libraries Montage pins.** OpenEXR 3.5.4 fixes two security advisories and a memory regression when writing; Montage pins v3.5.2 (`CMakeLists.txt`). ONNX Runtime 1.30.1 (29 October) fixes out-of-bounds reads, and 1.32.0 followed; Montage's Linux set-up fetches 1.30.0 (`scripts/setup-deps.sh`). libjxl 0.13.0 has security fixes; Montage takes libjxl from each platform's packages. All three go to #12.
- **The nightly review of 15 November.** It covered 123 commits (Phase 15 and the fixes of 10 November) and left some findings open on purpose. Per-shot ladders score each rung at its own size, cap only the trial encodes, cannot go below CRF 18, and make VMAF costly by default (#1). The focus check's grain estimate reads fine texture as grain (#12). The audio callback is still not real-time safe (#2).

Every candidate was checked against `README.md`, the source in `src/` and the `PROGRESS.md` checklists with their "Not done" and "Not yet" notes. These are already there and are not proposed again.

Ranking is value to a professional editor, colourist, sound editor or finishing artist × feasibility in C++/Qt/FFmpeg with optional local ONNX and GGUF models. Items 1–12 can be tested completely in CI and come first; 13–15 each have one part that cannot be checked here, said in the row. Each row ends with what is out of reach. Nothing below needs a paid specification, an account, a cloud API or an API key. No new model is proposed, and no closed SDK is used. Every dependency is GPL-3.0-compatible unless **flagged**. The licences below were read from each project's repository on 15 November, unless a line says otherwise:
- SVT-AV1, built at v4.3.0 for #6's tests: the BSD 3-Clause Clear License (`LICENSE.md`, Alliance for Open Media, 2021). It states that it grants no patent rights; its `PATENTS.md` was not opened. It adds no restriction GPL-3.0 forbids, as Phase 15 found for the same licence on VVenC. Montage's packages already carry SVT-AV1 inside FFmpeg.
- AVM: the BSD 3-Clause Clear License (`LICENSE` on GitHub's `main`), read for the first time. That settles AVM's own licence, for the owner to confirm. dav2d's `COPYING` on code.videolan.org was refused again (a bot check): **flag**, as in Phases 14 and 15. AV2 stays built but not packaged.
- `KHR_gaussian_splatting`, for #8: a specification, not code. Its page says it may be used and reproduced without fee or royalty and grants no patent or trademark licence.
- No new library is linked by #9: receiving WebRTC uses libdatachannel, which Montage already links for browser review.
- QEMU and a guest kernel, for #14's test: Ubuntu's packages, not built, not linked and not shipped. Their licences were not read: **flag**, test tools only.
- ONNX Runtime 1.32.0 and OpenEXR 3.5.4, for #12: newer versions of what is already used. Their licence files were not read again.
- The ASIO SDK: as in Phase 15, **flag**. No build has it.
- MXL (lower priority only): its licence was not read, **flag**.

Everything can be built and tested in the Linux cloud container (and on macOS and Windows in the Package workflow) except where a row says otherwise. One item needs a tool built in CI from source that is not built today: SVT-AV1 v4.3.0 (#6), from a pinned tag checked against its commit. The tools already approved stay as they are: AVM, dav2d, libvmaf v3.2.1, tinyusdz, Weston 16.0.2 with wayland-protocols 1.46, and SVT-JPEG-XS v0.10.0. #13 installs Qt 6.12 through aqtinstall (a download, not a build), and #14 installs QEMU from Ubuntu's packages. Sizes: S ≤ 2, M 2–6, L 6–12 person-weeks.

Phase 15 #10's notes: "A slot's output applies to recorded passes only: playing live, every slot is heard on the sampler's track", and drawn velocity curves "are set over MCP only". **Callback.** In `audio/AudioDevice`, on JACK and PortAudio alike, the callback takes no lock, allocates nothing and builds nothing. What the UI changes reaches it as snapshots swapped in one step and over queues that never wait. The mixer's buffers and every resampler are made ahead on a worker when the graph, the rate or the block size changes. The voiceover tap writes into a ring made before the take.
