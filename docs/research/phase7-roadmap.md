# Phase 7 roadmap (October 2026)

A sixth gap analysis, made as Phase 6 drew to a close. It compares Montage with Premiere 26.5 (to 26.5.2), DaVinci Resolve 21.1 and 21.1.1, Final Cut Pro 12.3 and 12.4 and Media Composer 2026.8. It also looks at Baselight v7, Pro Tools 2026.4, Descript and CapCut. Every candidate was checked against `README.md` and the `PROGRESS.md` checklists and their "Not done" notes. These are already there and are not proposed again: Match Voice (a static EQ match), Room Tone, De-Reverb, Spleeter stems, cuts built from a script (Final Draft included), spoken and OCR slates, planar tracking and Corner Pin, Lens Blur (Depth), Film Look's halation and grain, Object Removal, Blemish Remover, lensfun correction, Rolling Shutter Repair, capture of SRT, RIST and FFmpeg devices, IMF checked with Photon, the stills gallery, HDR trims, Dialog LKFS, CEA-708, binaural SOFA, the MCP agent link and Lua and Python scripting. Dolby Vision, per-bin locking and direct publishing stay out.

Ranking is value to a professional editor or colourist × feasibility in C++/Qt/FFmpeg with optional local ONNX models. Nothing below needs a paid SDK, an account, a cloud API or an API key. Every dependency and model is GPL-3.0-compatible unless **flagged**. Everything can be built and tested in the Linux cloud container except where a row says otherwise. Sizes: S ≤ 2, M 2–6, L 6–12 person-weeks.

## Earlier leftovers carried forward

- **GPU compositor** (Phase 6 #1): three parts are done. Still open: OCIO on the GPU, depth, people and object masks, Refine Edges, HDR Palette, the remaining effects, and YUV frames uploaded as decoded.
- **Blackmagic RAW** (Phase 6 #2) is still blocked on the SDK's terms.
- **Capture** (Phase 6 #13): DeckLink, AJA and NDI input, deck control and batch capture remain. **MV-HEVC out** (Phase 6 #15) is possible only through NVENC or Apple's own API. None of these can be tested here.
- Small Phase 6 remainders worth doing alongside: the dialogue-gated loudness range (LRA); roll-up, paint-on and service-2 708 captions (708 in an ST 436 track is now part of #10); Tangent controls for Curves, the Colour Warper and the HDR Palette; split screen across several stills.
- **Prompted sound isolation** still has no practical model with usable terms. SAM Audio's download is gated behind an account, and AudioSep was trained on WavCaps, which is academic-only. **Temporally consistent inpainting** is in the same position: ProPainter is non-commercial, and the diffusion removers are multi-gigabyte DiTs. Dialogue, music and effects separation (#3) covers the commonest need, getting dialogue out of a mix.

## Phase 7 candidates

| # | Feature | Who has it | Size | Notes |
|---|---------|-----------|------|-------|
| 1 | Script view with lined coverage: the screenplay (Final Draft, Fountain or text) as a panel. Each take is a vertical line down the lines it covers. Click a line to cue every take at that line. Circled takes, lines already in the cut ticked, uncovered lines flagged, and a lined-script PDF | Media Composer (Script Integration, ScriptSync AI), Resolve 21 (IntelliScript) | S–M | In-house, on what exists. `core/ScriptCut` already reads scripts (`parseScript`, `fdxToScript`) and aligns lines to transcript readings (`matchScript`, Smith–Waterman). Keep that alignment per take in the project (`Project::script`) instead of using it only to build a cut. Add a Fountain reader (an open plain-text syntax). `app/ScriptPanel` lays out pages with QTextDocument, one line per take coloured by camera or angle. A click plays that take from that line in the Source monitor; two takes go side by side in the existing two-up view. Circled takes come from the slate log. Editing the script re-aligns it. Export the PDF with QPdfWriter, and add MCP `montage_script`. Testable on the transcript fixtures |
| 2 | Dialogue match: make ADR or a pickup sound like the production take, matching room reverb, a tonal match that follows the reference over time, and level, with room tone under it; plus a convolution reverb | Resolve 20 (AI Dialogue Matcher), Resolve 21 (EQ Match, Level Matcher), Pro Tools with iZotope Dialogue Match; Final Cut 11.1 and Audition (convolution reverbs) | S–M | In-house, building on Match Voice (the 21-band fit to the Parametric EQ), De-Reverb's `estimateReverbTime` and Room Tone. Measure decay per octave (RT60) and the direct-to-reverberant ratio blind, from the reference's speech offsets. Build a room response as band-filtered, exponentially decaying noise after a direct impulse, or load the user's impulse-response WAV. A new keyframable Convolution Reverb effect does uniformly partitioned FFT convolution with the FFT Spectral Repair already uses. The dynamic match refits the EQ every 0.5 s and writes smoothed EQ keys; level comes from loudness, as in Auto Mix. Test: speech through a known synthetic room, recovered within ±15 % RT60 per band and 1 dB of spectrum |
| 3 | Dialogue, music and effects separation: split a finished mix that has no stems into dialogue, music and effects. Use it to build the M&E for foreign versions, to rebalance, or to take music out of a scene | Resolve 19+ (Dialogue Separator FX, Music Remixer), iZotope RX | M | Bandit v2 (Watcharasupat et al.). Its code is Apache-2.0 and its checkpoints are on Zenodo under CC BY-SA 4.0, which Creative Commons lists as one-way compatible with GPL-3.0; credit the authors. It was trained on DnR v3 (CC BY-SA 4.0), which uses only CC0, CC BY, CC BY-SA and Apache speech, FMA music and FSD50K effects, with the non-commercial parts excluded. There are English, French, German, Spanish, Mandarin, Faroese and multilingual models, 447 MB each. Export the band-split RNN to ONNX and host it pinned by revision and SHA-256, as the Spleeter graph is. Run it through `media/Inference` in overlapping chunks. The stems arrive linked on new tracks with the Dialogue, Music and Effects roles, and Export's extra audio streams write the M&E. A 2026 evaluation found music and effects weaker than dialogue on real film mixes, so say so in the UI |
| 4 | Timecode from audio (LTC): read SMPTE or EBU LTC from any channel, set it as the file's timecode with the original kept (audio-only files too), sync by it, silence the LTC channel; generate LTC | Resolve (Update Timecode from Audio Track); Tentacle Sync Studio and similar tools for Premiere, Final Cut and Avid | S | libltc (x42, LGPL-3.0; encodes and decodes, with date support). Decode on import or on request (media bin, MCP), choosing the channel with the most valid frames. Fit a line through the decoded frames to get the start timecode and drift, and warn when drift passes a frame. Store the result next to the file's own timecode, so proxies and relinks still match (Resolve overwrites it, which breaks them). Turn the LTC channel off in source channel mapping. Dual-system sound's sync by timecode then just works. Add an LTC generator clip. Test with libltc-encoded WAVs at 23.976–60 fps, drop-frame, with noise and at low and high levels |
| 5 | Live remote review: stream the Program monitor or the reference output to clients at low latency, 10-bit, with sound, burnt-in timecode and a passphrase; a Montage client can send markers back | Resolve Studio (Remote Monitoring, through a Blackmagic Cloud account), Evercast and Streambox (paid services) | S–M | FFmpeg only. Encode the frames the viewer shows (hardware H.264/HEVC where present, else x264/x265) with zero-latency settings and send them over SRT (FFmpeg's libsrt, MPL-2.0, AES with a passphrase) as listener or caller. Viewers watch in Montage (its capture already reads SRT), VLC or ffplay. Optionally push WebRTC through FFmpeg 8's WHIP muxer (experimental) to a self-hosted WHIP/WHEP server such as MediaMTX (MIT), so clients can watch in a browser. No relay or account of Montage's own. Markers return as JSON lines on a TCP side channel. Tested entirely on loopback: sent, received by Montage's own capture, frames and timecode checked |
| 6 | Gyro stabilisation from camera motion data (GoPro, Sony, DJI, Insta360, Canon, Blackmagic RAW, phone loggers), with rolling-shutter correction from the same data and horizon lock | Resolve (Camera Gyro mode for Blackmagic cameras), Sony Catalyst Browse, Gyroflow's plugins (GPL-3.0) in Resolve, Premiere and Final Cut | M | gpmf-parser (GoPro; C; MIT or Apache-2.0; its sample clips serve as tests) and telemetry-parser (Rust; MIT or Apache-2.0; Sony rtmd, DJI, Insta360, Canon, BRAW, CAMM, Betaflight, .gcsv), the latter behind a small C ABI built the way c2pa-rs is. Integrate the gyro into orientations. Find the gyro-to-video offset by correlating with the rotation the point tracker measures. Smooth with the stabiliser's existing smoothing, plus a horizon lock from the accelerometer. Warp each row by its own orientation, using the readout time Rolling Shutter Repair already has and the lens from lensfun or a Gyroflow lens profile (JSON; GPL-3.0 repository). gyroflow-core (GPL-3.0, compatible) is a usable reference. The stabiliser offers a Camera Gyro mode only when data is found |
| 7 | Restoration: automatic dirt and dust removal, a dust-busting brush, vertical scratch removal, a tracked patch replacer, and clone or heal paint for rigs, logos and boom shadows | Resolve (Automatic Dirt Removal, Dust Buster, Patch Replacer, Fusion Paint), Media Composer (Paint, Scratch Removal), Baselight (Paint) | M | In-house. Dirt is a pixel that differs from both motion-compensated neighbours in the same direction, a spike test on the optical flow retiming already computes. Each defect is grown and filled from those neighbours, with size and strength limits and a Show Detected view. Scratches are thin, persistent columns found in a high-passed column profile and filled across. The patch replacer copies a source region (shape mask, carried by the planar tracker) onto a target and blends it in the gradient domain (Poisson), so the lighting matches. Clone and heal strokes are drawn in the Program monitor, on one frame or riding a track. Test with synthetic specks and scratches removed and nothing else changed, and a logo covered with no seam |
| 8 | Synthetic focus: click to focus, keyed rack focus or focus that follows a tracked face or object, aperture shapes (blade count and rotation, anamorphic oval, cat's-eye toward the edges), highlight bloom, focus breathing, and depth haze | Resolve 21 (CineFocus), Final Cut 12.4 (Cinematic focus points), Baselight v7 (Depth Map, Bokeh, Haze) | M | Extends Lens Blur (Depth). Depth Anything V2 Small (Apache-2.0) is already there; steady it over time with the flow. Replace the five blur levels with a per-pixel circle of confusion from focus distance and f-number, gathered over depth slices front to back, so a foreground edge blurs over the background without haloes. Apply the aperture kernel in scene-linear light so highlights form bokeh. Pick the focus point by clicking the depth map, hold it on a YuNet face or SAM 2 object track, and key eased racks. Haze is exp(−β·depth) toward a colour, with a height falloff. Phase 6's lower-priority "click-to-focus" now has a route |
| 9 | Multi-layer EXR in and out: choose a layer (AOV) per clip, pick Cryptomatte mattes by clicking objects, write the clip's mattes and layers into VFX pulls; HTJ2K compression in EXR and in IMF | Resolve (Fusion Cryptomatte; 21.1 EXR with HTJ2K), Baselight v7 (Cryptomatte channel selection), Nuke; Blender and most renderers write Cryptomatte | S–M | OpenEXR 3.4+ (BSD-3), which brings OpenJPH (BSD-2) for HTJ2K. Read multi-part and multi-layer files (`layer.R` channel names) and offer the layers in Interpret Footage. Cryptomatte follows its open specification (Psyop, BSD-3): ranked ID and coverage pairs, a manifest in the header, MurmurHash3 (public domain) names. A click in the monitor adds that object to the clip's mask as one more object (`core/ObjectMask`). EXR pulls gain `htj2k256`/`htj2k32`. For IMF App 2E HTJ2K (ST 2067-21:2023 Annex I), use OpenJPH beside OpenJPEG with RPCL order, checked with Photon 4.9.9+ as the IMF tests already are |
| 10 | Broadcast MXF by the book: AMWA AS-11 (UK DPP HD and the other shims) with core and DPP descriptive metadata and part segmentation from markers; RDD 9 XDCAM HD422; CEA-708 and OP-47 teletext subtitles in an ST 436 ANC track | Premiere and Media Encoder (AS-11 presets with DPP metadata), Media Composer (AS-11 export); Resolve only through the paid MainConcept plugin | S–M | bmx (BBC, now maintained at EBU; BSD-3; C++ over libMXF). Montage's encoders make the essence (AVC-Intra 100 through x264, MPEG-2 4:2:2, PCM) and bmx wraps it. It writes AS-11 core and UK DPP metadata from a form in the export dialog (title, series, episode, line-up, audio layout, subtitles…), with parts taken from markers flagged Part, and follows RDD 9's index and partition rules. ST 436 VANC carries 708 cc_data (closing Phase 6 #7's leftover) and OP-47 from the caption track. UK broadcasters require AS-11 UK DPP HD. Tests read files back with bmx's `mxf2raw` and MediaInfo (BSD-2) |
| 11 | Monitor calibration: a calibration 3D LUT per display and per output (GUI viewer, full-screen, SDI or NDI reference, HDR viewer), and a network patch generator so calibration software can measure a display through Montage | Resolve (monitor LUTs; a patch generator for CalMAN, ColourSpace and LightSpace), Baselight v7 (device colour spaces) | S | In-house. The LUT (.cube, .3dl or CLF, through the existing readers) is applied last, after the output transform, and never goes into exports or exported LUTs. It is chosen per screen (QScreen name) and per output. The patch generator speaks the XML-over-TCP "Resolve" protocol (port 20002) that DisplayCAL (GPL-3.0) and PGenerator+ implement, showing RGB patches at a bit depth and size, full screen or on the reference output. **Measuring needs a probe** (ArgyllCMS, AGPL-3.0, run as its own program), so it cannot be tested here; the protocol is tested against a fake server |
| 12 | Sky replacement: find the sky, key it with refined edges, put in a still or moving sky that follows the camera, and match the foreground's colour to it | Resolve 21.1 (Sky Replacement, now for photos as well) | S–M | No new model. Depth Anything V2 Small marks the far, upper regions. Seeds taken from them go to SAM 2 (Apache-2.0, already used), which follows the sky through the shot, and Refine Edges handles branches and hair. Camera motion from the point tracker, or a homography of distant features (camera solve exists), moves the new sky. Match Colour and a light wrap tie the foreground in, and a horizon blur hides the join. Tested on a rendered scene with a known sky |
| 13 | Face reshaping and eye contact: slim or widen the face, jaw, nose, eyes and mouth; turn a presenter's gaze to the lens when they read from a script | Resolve 21 (Face Reshaper), CapCut (face reshape, eye contact), Descript (Eye Contact), NVIDIA Broadcast | M | MediaPipe Face Landmarker (FaceMesh-V2: 478 points including irises; its model cards say Apache-2.0), converted from TFLite to ONNX with tf2onnx (Apache-2.0) and hosted pinned. YuNet still finds the faces. Reshaping moves landmark groups by sliders and warps by moving least squares over the canonical mesh's triangles, smoothed over time so faces do not swim. For eye contact, take the head pose from the mesh and shift each iris inside its eyelid aperture toward the camera axis with a local warp, leaving blinks alone. It handles small angles only (a prompter or a script below the lens) and fades out past a limit. Test: the JFK portrait reshaped by a known amount; synthetic irises moved |
| 14 | Parameter expressions and links: drive any keyframable parameter with a short formula (time, frame, wiggle, loops, another clip's parameter, an audio level), and bake it to keys | Fusion (Resolve), After Effects, Motion (behaviours) | S | Lua 5.4 is already embedded (`automation/ScriptHost`). Use one sandboxed state per render thread: each expression is compiled once per parameter and evaluated per frame, with no I/O and seeded noise, so renders repeat exactly. Helpers: `time`, `frame`, `value` (the keyed value), `wiggle`, `loopOut`, `param(clip, name)`, `audio(track)` (the envelope that audio-reactive motion uses). Expressions are saved in projects and drawn as curves in the Keyframes panel. Bake them to keys for interchange. An error shows in the Inspector, and the keyed value is used instead |
| 15 | Lip-synced dubbing: re-render the mouth to match a dub or regenerated words | Descript (translation with lip sync), CapCut, HeyGen | M–L | MuseTalk 1.5 (Tencent). The code is MIT, and its README says the weights are "available for any purpose, even commercially". Its parts: sd-vae-ft-mse (MIT), whisper-tiny (MIT), BiSeNet face parsing (MIT); DWPose can be replaced by the mesh from #13. **Flag:** it was trained on HDTF (videos collected from YouTube) and a private set, so the training data's terms are unclear. It works on a 256×256 mouth region with a Stable-Diffusion-sized UNet, so it is slow on a CPU: render offline (Render and Replace) and use the GPU providers where present. Ask for consent as voice cloning does, and add a C2PA assertion that the picture was altered. Dub and Regenerate Words feed it |

Lower priority: a stills workflow like Resolve 21's Photo page (albums graded and exported in a batch; tethered capture stays out); DNG opcode lists (Resolve 21.1); deck control over Sony 9-pin RS-422 with batch capture from a log (testable against a pseudo-terminal); printer-points offset for colourists from film; Resolve 21.1.1's asynchronous script calls; splitting one export across render nodes; keys for up to 25 multicam angles (Resolve 21.1); face ageing (needs a generative model).

Left out: generated video, music and sound effects (Premiere 26.5's Generative Media Tool, Descript's AI music and effects, CapCut's video models) because they run on cloud models with credits, and the open alternatives are non-commercial (MMAudio's weights are CC BY-NC 4.0); MPEG-H authoring (Pro Tools 2026.4) because Fraunhofer's renderer is free but proprietary (**flag**); Dolby headphone personalisation (a Sonarworks phone app; Montage already takes any personal SOFA file); Avid Content Core, the Gemini panel and Blackmagic Cloud presentations (accounts).
