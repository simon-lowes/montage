- **Baselight 7.0.4 (23 November).** Digital Production's article of 25 November lists:
  - Consolidate and Transcode, in place of its Consolidate View: media copied, transcoded to image sequences or transcoded to movies, for the scene, the open scenes or chosen scenes. Output in a chosen format and colour space or each shot's own. Handles left out, set in frames or a timecode duration, or taken whole. Output names and folders from substitutions, folders kept to a chosen depth. Copies of movies trimmed where the format allows, and formats that cannot be trimmed transcoded. Shots that add nothing to the output left out. The grade baked in, or only the input processed. Jobs sent to another machine's render queue.
  - Hierarchical presets for these operations: factory, site, job and scene, each overriding the broader ones, with a compare view that shows the differences from the next level up.
  - In Timeline Sort, handles that can be scaled with a shot's speed, and shots at another frame rate treated as speed changes.
  - A Rendering Accuracy setting: more accurate RGB and Y′CbCr conversion for 4:2:2 and 4:2:0, more precise legal and full range conversion, and options to clip negative values or clip to full scale in floating-point deliverables, OpenEXR among them.
  - Canon RAW's camera roll and tilt carried into OpenEXR deliverables, and OpenEXR camera metadata written as float rather than double attributes "for Nuke compatibility".
  - Explicit versions of a shot, which move with the shot when it is slipped or trimmed. A LUT or a grade stack fitted as editable grading operators by a local model (Chromogen ML). Leica L-Log, and LibRaw 0.22.1.

- **SMPTE's engineering report on provenance (17 November).** SMPTE's post of 19 November says the report, from a survey of 127 professionals, asks that provenance data be kept and updatable through editing, transcoding, repackaging, distribution and archiving; that it work within existing formats, "including MXF", without changes to the core MXF standard; that it move between systems without loss; that it be aligned with C2PA; and that identifying details can be redacted to protect contributors. The report itself (a PDF linked from the post) was not opened. Montage signs exports with Content Credentials, but only in the formats c2pa-rs embeds in: `credentialFormat` in `media/ContentCredentials.cpp` names MP4, MOV, M4A, JPEG, PNG, TIFF, DNG, WebP, HEIF, AVIF, WAV, MP3, AVI, PDF and SVG, and every other export is refused a signature. Neither `render/ProjectManager.cpp` nor the proxy maker touches credentials, so a consolidated copy of a camera-signed file loses its signature, and so does everything exported from it after (#6).
- **Consolidate in Montage.** `ConsolidateOptions` (`render/ProjectManager.h`) has one choice of codec for trimmed copies, `prores_ks` or `libx264`, and handles in seconds. Every trimmed copy is coded again, even a ProRes or DNxHR source that Montage's own smart rendering could cut at any frame. Only videos are trimmed (`ConsolidateResult::trimmed`), there is no image-sequence output, no naming pattern, no colour space to choose, no grade to bake, and no saved presets. Baselight 7.0.4 made all of these one tool (#1).
