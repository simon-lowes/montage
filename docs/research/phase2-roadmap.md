# Twenty builds close Montage's professional gap

Montage already has the classic editorial baseline that Premiere Pro, DaVinci Resolve, Final Cut Pro and Media Composer were built on: timeline, trimming, keyframes, colour correction, mixer and multi-codec export. What it lacks is the layer the four leaders added in 2024–2026, which has three parts: **trust and throughput** (crash-safe saving, hardware decoding, GPU compositing); a **speech layer**, where one transcription engine feeds captions, phrase search, text-based editing and automatic multicam cuts; and **finishing depth** (colour management with ACES 2.0, masks and tracking, and audio plugins with editors, buses and offline rendering). Professionals adopt "utility AI" (transcription, speech cleanup, object masks) and dismiss generative B-roll, so Montage should ship local, open models (whisper.cpp, SAM 2, DeepFilterNet, RIFE) and leave cloud generation to others. Every one of the twenty Phase 2 items can be built on MIT, BSD, ISC, Apache or LGPL code; Steinberg's move of the VST3 SDK to MIT in October 2025 removed the last licence obstacle to plugin hosting. Montage's new plugin scanner already follows Ardour's documented practice of out-of-process probing, a blocklist and a cache keyed by file identity, so the plugin work left is VST3, AU and LV2 hosting, editor windows, a manager UI, and track/bus effect chains. The main uncertainty is that no quantitative 2024–2026 survey ranks NLE features: the impact rankings below synthesise switching stories, the features every vendor converged on, and complaints about open-source editors.

## Stability, playback and transcripts outrank every headline feature

No independent, methodologically sound NLE market-share or feature-priority survey for 2024–2026 exists in the research; aggregator figures such as "Premiere 35%, FCP 25%, Resolve 15%" give no methodology ([ElectroIQ](https://electroiq.com/stats/video-editing-statistics/)). The ranking therefore weighs three signals: how often a feature appears in published switching stories, whether all four leaders shipped it in 2024–2026, and whether open-source editors are publicly criticised for lacking it.

Trust comes first. Both detailed 2025–2026 accounts of editors leaving Premiere lead with crashes, render artefacts and sync bugs, not missing features. One switcher wrote she was "tired of getting slowed down by a constantly crashing software" and praised Resolve's Live Save: "I never fear that something is gone!" ([Digital Production, Mar 2026](https://digitalproduction.com/2026/03/18/getting-your-bearings-switching-from-premiere-pro-to-davinci-resolve/)). Another listed waveform misalignment that survived cache clearing, alongside a **$6,598.80 ten-year Creative Cloud bill versus $299 for Resolve Studio** ([Digital Production, Sep 2025](https://digitalproduction.com/2025/09/01/goodbye-adobe-premiere/)). Open-source editors fail the same test: Kdenlive users report crashes that "destroy your work" and corrupted backup saves ([Hacker News](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47815118)).

Playback performance is the second gate, and vendors price it accordingly. Resolve reserves advanced hardware H.264/H.265 decode and encode, full GPU optimisation and anything above UHD 60p for the paid Studio edition ([Blackmagic Studio](https://www.blackmagicdesign.com/products/davinciresolve/studio)). Final Cut Pro moved its proxies to HEVC in 12.3 ([Apple release notes](https://support.apple.com/en-us/102825)), Avid extended dual-resolution proxy linking to its Ultimate tier in 2025.6 ([Motion Media](https://www.motionmedia.com/mm-blog/avid-media-composer-20256-whats-new-in-the-latest-version-of-the-industryleading-editing-software/)), and Kdenlive's own short-term roadmap puts GPU decoding, 10/12-bit colour and GPU effects at the top ([Kdenlive roadmap](https://kdenlive.org/roadmap/)).

Transcripts became the third pillar in this cycle. The 2026 Best Film Editing winner credited Avid's automatic transcription: "When you need to find just a word or a phrase you can search and find it in all the different takes" ([Avid via BroadcastBeat](https://broadcastbeat.com/news/celebrating-the-greatest-creators-one-battle-after-another-and-f1-among-majority-of-oscar-wins-created-using-avid)). The European Accessibility Act has required captions on covered audiovisual content since **28 June 2025** ([Wistia](https://wistia.com/learn/marketing/european-accessibility-act)), and every leader now generates captions automatically.

### Utility AI wins; generative AI stays on the margins

All four leaders spent 2025–2026 on AI, and their newest features cluster in the same places. Adobe splits its work between local analysis and cloud generation. Locally, Premiere 26.0 (20 January 2026) made the click-to-select, tracked **Object Mask** its headline and made shape masks track up to 20× faster ([ProVideo Coalition](https://www.provideocoalition.com/new-ai-and-masking-tools-in-premiere-plus-major-upgrade-to-after-e%ef%ac%80ects/)); Media Intelligence search runs entirely on the user's machine ([ProVideo Coalition](https://www.provideocoalition.com/unlocking-efficiency-with-media-intelligence-in-adobe-premiere-pro/)); and a Speechmatics on-device model transcribes **an hour of audio in about 55 seconds, within 5% of cloud accuracy** ([Podnews](https://podnews.net/press-release/adobe-speechmatics-on-device)). In the cloud, Generative Extend runs against generative credits, and the conversational **Creative Agent**, in public beta since 18 June 2026, builds bins, transcripts, stringouts and rough cuts under daily credits, which implies cloud processing ([Adobe Community](https://community.adobe.com/announcements-727/adobe-since-nab-2026-what-editors-should-know-1629489); [Adobe Community thread](https://community.adobe.com/questions-729/when-does-premiere-process-it-s-ai-tasks-on-device-or-in-the-cloud-1553097)).

Apple runs everything on-device. Final Cut Pro 12.0 added Transcript Search, Visual Search and Beat Detection, and 12.3 (30 June 2026) added Generate Captions, Auto Mask, a rebuilt Match Color and Edit Detection; the language features are US-English-only, and the set needs Apple silicon ([Apple release notes](https://support.apple.com/en-us/102825); [Camera Jabber](https://camerajabber.com/photography-news/final-cut-pro-adds-ai-generated-captions-and-edit-detection-in-major-update/)). Blackmagic shipped the widest range. Resolve 21 (2 June 2026) added IntelliSearch, a Speech Generator that clones a voice from a roughly 10-second sample, CineFocus, three face tools, Slate ID, UltraSharpen and Motion Deblur ([Blackmagic What's New](https://www.blackmagicdesign.com/products/davinciresolve/whatsnew)). Resolve 21.1 (9 September 2026) added a Studio-only **MCP server** that lets Claude, Claude Code and ChatGPT Codex drive Resolve, and in the same release moved Python scripting to Studio ([CineD](https://www.cined.com/davinci-resolve-21-1-released-ai-assistant-integration-via-mcp-individual-hdr-trims-and-python-scripting-moves-to-studio/)). The hardware bar is high: Resolve's AI tools require **16 GB of VRAM on Windows and Linux** ([Blackmagic readme](https://www.blackmagicdesign.com/support/content/readme/5e8ed1f827564634984496004809d558)). Avid's 2026.8 release (1 September 2026) is narrower and centred on dialogue. It adds **PhraseFindAI Multicam Auto Cut**, which maps transcript speakers to camera angles, plus an under-documented "Ready to Edit" agent and transcript versioning ([RedShark](https://www.redsharknews.com/media-composer-2026-8-features)). A Google Gemini panel, from a partnership announced in April 2026, appeared only as an IBC tech preview ([Google Cloud](https://www.googlecloudpresscorner.com/2026-04-16-Avid-and-Google-Cloud-Announce-Partnership-to-Bring-Agentic-AI-to-Media-Production); [RedShark](https://www.redsharknews.com/media-composer-2026-8-features)).

## Montage matches the 2015 baseline but misses the 2024–2026 layer

Montage's README and source show a credible MVP. Editing covers a multi-track timeline with ripple, roll, slip, slide and razor, three-point editing, compound clips, audio-waveform sync and scene detection. Picture runs through a multi-threaded 32-bit float CPU compositor with 13 blend modes, primary correction, curves, LUTs, keys and transitions; audio has a mixer with EQ, compressor and limiter plus BS.1770 loudness normalisation; and media handling includes 960 px proxies, EDL and hand-written OTIO export, and a broad FFmpeg export set that builds and passes its tests against **FFmpeg 9.0.2** ([release tarball](https://ffmpeg.org/releases/ffmpeg-9.0.2.tar.xz)). The same source shows the gaps. There is no FFmpeg hwaccel code and no QRhi or OpenGL code; there are no masks, tracking or stabilisation, and speed changes are constant-rate only. Audio tracks carry only a fader and pan, with no effect chains or buses. Saves are atomic through `QSaveFile`, but only a single `.autosave` copy is written every two minutes. CLAP plugins run only as stereo clip effects with no editor window, and no part of the Qt app exposes the plugin registry.

Impact below is scored 1–5: a 5 is table stakes that appears in switching stories and that all four leaders ship, a 4 is a differentiator shipped by at least three leaders, a 3 is segment-specific, a 2 is niche, and a 1 is dismissed by professionals. Effort is in person-weeks for one experienced contributor who knows the codebase (**S ≤ 2, M 2–6, L 6–12, XL > 12**). Both scales are engineering judgements, not measurements.

The ranking is not a pure impact-to-effort ratio; it also weighs what each item unlocks. **The transcription engine (#4)** is the highest-leverage build in the plan, because one whisper.cpp integration powers captions (#5), phrase search and text-based editing (#7), and speaker-based multicam cutting (#14), features the leaders sell separately. **The plugin manager UI (#3)** ranks high despite modest impact because it is small and makes today's CLAP work usable at all, and VST3 (#6) follows quickly because it carries the largest commercial catalogue. **The GPU compositor (#15)** has top-tier impact but sits mid-list as the largest item: hardware decode (#2), together with Montage's existing decode-at-display-size and render-ahead cache, captures much of the playback win sooner, and the GPU path's payoff grows as masks, OCIO and AI mattes stack up. Each deferred item waits for a reason: OpenFX needs the GPU compositor for its 1.5 GPU suites, collaboration and AAF export are XL projects that serve the Avid segment, visual search lacks a researched and licence-checked model, and generative features carry cloud costs, large models and the professional distrust documented above.

The licence posture is already set. Montage's export presets link GPL x264 and x265, and under FFmpeg's rules "the GPL applies to all of FFmpeg" in such builds ([FFmpeg legal](https://ffmpeg.org/legal.html)). Because the roadmap adds Apache-2.0 components (OpenCV, OTIO, sherpa-onnx, SAM 2), and Apache-2.0 is incompatible with GPLv2-only code, the cleanest choice is to declare Montage **GPL-3.0-or-later** (the repository has no LICENSE file today) and to avoid GPL-2.0-only dependencies. The GPL-only pieces (x264/x265, frei0r, LibAAF, RobustVideoMatting, Gyroflow) should stay separable; every other library chosen below is permissive or LGPL, so an LGPL build using OS encoders remains possible for a future app-store route. Three dependencies need care. Avoid JUCE, which is offered under AGPLv3 or a commercial EULA ([JUCE forum](https://forum.juce.com/t/amendments-to-the-juce-end-user-licence-agreement-for-juce-8/61265)). Avoid SAM 3, which ships under Meta's custom licence with anti-reverse-engineering and military-use clauses ([SAM 3 LICENSE](https://github.com/facebookresearch/sam3/blob/main/LICENSE)). And treat the GPL hosts Carla and Ardour as reading material only ([Carla README](https://github.com/falkTX/Carla/blob/main/README.md)), because copying their code would tie Montage to their exact licence terms.

## Twenty implementations on mostly MIT, BSD and Apache code

### Trust and throughput

**#1 Crash-safe project history and recovery (S).** Montage already writes saves atomically through `QSaveFile` and offers to restore a newer `.autosave`; Phase 2 should add four things. Because edits are pure functions over a value-type `Project` with snapshot undo, a debounced live save after every committed edit is cheap. A ring of the last N timestamped snapshots in a backups folder means one corrupt write cannot destroy the only copy. A session lock file detects an unclean exit, so the next launch can offer recovery from the newest good snapshot. Finally, a plugin safe mode starts with third-party plugins bypassed when the previous session crashed with them active, mirroring Shotcut 26.6, which restarts without external plugins after a crash within 30 seconds of launch ([Shotcut forum](https://forum.shotcut.org/t/external-plugin-support-openfx-vst2-and-lv2/51741)). None of this needs a new dependency, and it answers the most cited reason editors leave a tool.

**#2 Hardware decode and encode (M).** FFmpeg now covers every platform API: 6.1 added Vulkan decoding of H.264, HEVC and AV1 ([Phoronix](https://www.phoronix.com/news/FFmpeg-6.1-Released)), 8.0 added Vulkan VP9 decoding and AV1 encoding, and 8.1 added D3D12 H.264/AV1 encoding ([FFmpeg news](https://ffmpeg.org/index.html)); Montage already targets 9.0.2. The decode path probes `avcodec_get_hw_config()` per stream and creates the platform device (VideoToolbox, D3D11VA/D3D12VA, VAAPI, NVDEC/CUDA or Vulkan). Its `get_format` callback falls back to software when the hardware format is not offered, which is common for 4:2:2 and 4:4:4 profiles, and until #15 lands, frames are downloaded to system memory for the CPU compositor. Concurrent hardware decoders need a cap, because multicam and scrubbing open many, and frame-accurate seeking must be tested for parity against the software path. Encoding adds "fast" presets for `*_videotoolbox`, `*_nvenc`, `*_qsv`, `*_amf`, `*_vaapi`, `*_vulkan` and `*_d3d12va` beside x264/x265, which also avoid GPL encoders for those exports. Qt Multimedia's FFmpeg backend handles all of these hardware types and converts decoded frames into GPU textures ([Qt docs](https://doc.qt.io/qt-6/advanced-ffmpeg-configuration.html)); treat it as a design reference, not code to copy.

**#15 GPU compositor on QRhi (XL).** QRhi is the only cross-API GPU layer that ships with Qt. It covers Metal, D3D11/D3D12, Vulkan and OpenGL, supports compute where the API allows (OpenGL needs 4.3+), and has been usable by applications since Qt 6.6, but with "no source or binary compatibility guarantees" and private-module linkage ([Qt QRhi](https://doc.qt.io/qt-6/qrhi.html)). Montage must therefore raise its floor from Qt 6.4 to at least 6.6 and pin the Qt minor per release branch. Homebrew already ships Qt 6.11.2, so macOS is unaffected, but the Ubuntu 24.04 build, which relies on the distro's Qt 6.4, will need a newer Qt. The design uses RGBA16F intermediates (RGBA32F for accumulation-heavy effects) and one pass per effect. Built-in shaders are baked offline with `qsb`/`qt_add_shaders`, and `QShaderBaker` runs at runtime only for OCIO-generated code ([Qt QShaderBaker](https://doc.qt.io/qt-6/qshaderbaker.html)). Decode becomes zero-copy: VideoToolbox CVPixelBuffers become Metal textures wrapped by `QRhiTexture::createFrom`, D3D11VA decodes on QRhi's own device, and VAAPI dma-bufs are imported on Linux. The viewer gains an HDR10 or scRGB swapchain with SDR fallback ([Qt QRhiSwapChain](https://doc.qt.io/qt-6/qrhiswapchain.html)). The open-source record is a warning: Shotcut hid its GPU effects for years "due to instability" ([Shotcut forum](https://forum.shotcut.org/t/clarification-on-what-gpu-acceleration-means/10772/2)), Kdenlive users report GPU gains vanish once CPU effects join the chain ([KDE forum](https://forum.kde.org/viewtopic.php%3Ff=265&t=125953.html)), and Olive remains alpha ([Olive](https://github.com/olive-editor/olive)). Two rules follow. Every built-in effect needs a GPU port, so no chain mixes CPU and GPU passes. And the CPU float compositor stays as the reference, tested against GPU output with a tolerance; Shotcut 25.12 shipped its GPU mode as experimental and preserved linear light across the GPU-to-CPU handoff ([AlternativeTo](https://alternativeto.net/news/2026/1/shotcut-25-12-adds-10-bit-video-cpu-pipeline-linear-color-processing-and-ui-upgrades)).

### The speech layer

**#4 Transcription engine (M).** whisper.cpp is the in-process choice. It is MIT, as are OpenAI's Whisper weights ([openai/whisper](https://github.com/openai/whisper)), and it offers a C API, Metal, CUDA, Vulkan, Core ML and OpenVINO backends, Silero VAD, word-level timestamps, and models from 75 MiB (tiny) to 2.9 GiB (large) ([whisper.cpp](https://github.com/ggml-org/whisper.cpp)). The pipeline resamples audio to 16 kHz mono with libswresample and runs whisper.cpp on a worker thread with progress and abort callbacks. Models download on first use and are verified by hash, and each media item gets a stored transcript (words, source times, confidence, speaker) in the project cache. For speaker labels, sherpa-onnx (Apache-2.0) runs pyannote segmentation-3.0 (MIT) with speaker embeddings and clustering through a C API, with no Python ([sherpa-onnx](https://github.com/k2-fsa/sherpa-onnx); [Praat manual](https://www.fon.hum.uva.nl/praat/manual/pyannote_audio_MIT_License.html)). The stronger pyannote community-1 model is CC-BY-4.0 and gated, and needs attribution ([Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/pyannote/speaker-diarization-community-1)). FFmpeg's LGPL `whisper` filter suits `montage-cli` batch jobs ([af_whisper.c](https://github.com/FFmpeg/FFmpeg/blob/master/libavfilter/af_whisper.c)).

**#5 Caption tracks and delivery (M).** A new caption track type holds text, timing, style and position. Captions are generated from word timings under configurable line-length, characters-per-second and duration rules, with styles that include word-by-word highlighting, which Premiere 26.3 and Resolve 20 both ship ([Adobe Community](https://community.adobe.com/announcements-727/adobe-since-nab-2026-what-editors-should-know-1629489); [Newsshooter](https://www.newsshooter.com/2025/05/27/davinci-resolve-20-final-release/)). Burn-in reuses the existing title renderer. SRT and VTT sidecars are simple to write directly, and SCC and MCC can use FFmpeg's `scc`/`mcc` muxers ([allformats.c](https://github.com/FFmpeg/FFmpeg/blob/master/libavformat/allformats.c)). Embedded CEA-608 needs more, because FFmpeg has a 608 decoder but no text-to-608 encoder ([allcodecs.c](https://github.com/FFmpeg/FFmpeg/blob/master/libavcodec/allcodecs.c)). Montage should generate 608 byte pairs with libcaption (MIT) and attach them as `AV_FRAME_DATA_A53_CC` side data, so that libx264, libx265, NVENC and VideoToolbox write the SEI themselves ([libx264.c](https://github.com/FFmpeg/FFmpeg/blob/master/libavcodec/libx264.c)); ProRes deliverables get a MOV `c608` track ([movenc.c](https://github.com/FFmpeg/FFmpeg/blob/master/libavformat/movenc.c)). libcaption supports pop-on 608 only, its 708 output just carries 608 data in NTSC field 1, and its B-frame support is minimal ([libcaption](https://github.com/szatmary/libcaption)), so roll-up captions and true 708 come later.

**#7 Transcript panel: phrase search and text-based editing (M).** Built on #4, the panel shows each clip's transcript with speaker labels and click-to-seek. Selecting words sets source In/Out for insert or overwrite, the workflow Avid's Transcript Tool introduced in 2024.6 ([ProNews](https://en.pronews.com/news/2024042313097433.html)), and deleting words performs a ripple delete on the timeline, matching Resolve 19's text-based timeline editing ([Newsshooter](https://www.newsshooter.com/2024/08/21/davinci-resolve-19-final-release/)). A project-wide phrase search runs over an index in the project cache, and the original machine transcript is kept alongside user corrections, as Avid 2026.8 does ([ProVideo Coalition](https://www.provideocoalition.com/avid-media-composer-2026-8-is-now-available/)).

**#8 Dialogue denoise and voice isolation (M).** This ships in two tiers. The first exposes FFmpeg's `arnndn` as a clip effect; it derives from RNNoise under a BSD-style licence and is not in FFmpeg's GPL-only list ([RNNoise COPYING](https://github.com/xiph/rnnoise/blob/main/COPYING); [af_arnndn.c](https://github.com/FFmpeg/FFmpeg/blob/master/libavfilter/af_arnndn.c)). The second adds DeepFilterNet3 (MIT/Apache-2.0) at 48 kHz through ONNX Runtime (MIT), rendered and cached per clip ([DeepFilterNet](https://github.com/Rikorose/DeepFilterNet); [onnxruntime](https://github.com/microsoft/onnxruntime)). ONNX Runtime should use its CoreML provider on macOS and CUDA/TensorRT on NVIDIA; on Windows, plan around DirectML's "maintenance mode" status, since Microsoft now points to Windows ML on Windows 11 24H2 and later ([ONNX Runtime DirectML EP](https://onnxruntime.ai/docs/execution-providers/DirectML-ExecutionProvider.html)). Demucs stem separation (MIT) can come later; its repository says it "is not maintained anymore" ([Demucs](https://github.com/facebookresearch/demucs)).

### Audio plugins and mixing

**#3 Plugin manager UI (S).** Preferences › Audio Plug-ins sits on the existing `plugins::Registry`. It lists format, name, vendor, version, category and status, with a persisted enable checkbox per plugin, as in Premiere's Audio Plug-in Manager ([FXFactory](https://support.fxfactory.com/article/164-enable-audio-plug-ins-in-premiere-pro)). Its scan actions, "Scan for new", "Rescan selected" and "Full rescan including blocked", mirror Logic's Reset & Rescan ([Apple Support](https://support.apple.com/en-ng/122179)) and Ableton's deep rescan ([Sonnox](https://support.sonnox.com/support/solutions/articles/22000201335-re-scanning-plug-ins-in-ableton-live)). A blocked tab shows each reason with a Retry button, per-format folders are edited through `setSearchPaths`, and a scan log records each probe. At start-up the list fills from the cache immediately while scanning continues in the background, so a slow plugin never blocks launch, and the effects browser gains an "Audio Plug-ins" group.

**#6 VST3 hosting (M).** Steinberg moved the VST 3.8 SDK to the MIT licence on 29 October 2025, and the parts a host compiles (`pluginterfaces`, `base`, `public.sdk`) are each MIT ([vst3sdk LICENSE](https://github.com/steinbergmedia/vst3sdk/blob/master/LICENSE.txt); [Steinberg press release](https://ocl-steinberg-live.steinberg.net/_storage/asset/819253/storage/master/Press%20Release%20-%202025-10-29%20-%20VST%203.8%20-%20EN.pdf)). Montage should pin those three repositories to a v3.8.x tag with FetchContent, compile only the hosting sources rather than the SDK's example and validator build, and use the hosting `Module` class for loading and `moduleinfo.json` reading ([module.h](https://github.com/steinbergmedia/vst3_public_sdk/blob/master/source/vst/hosting/module.h)). The `plugins::Instance` implementation wraps `IComponent`, `IAudioProcessor` and `IEditController`. It calls `setupProcessing` with sample rate, maximum block, 32-bit samples and realtime or offline mode ([ivstaudioprocessor.h](https://github.com/steinbergmedia/vst3_pluginterfaces/blob/master/vst/ivstaudioprocessor.h)), negotiates stereo buses first, and translates keyframes into `IParameterChanges` points at sample offsets. It reports `getLatencySamples` and `getTailSamples`, and saves both the component and controller state as base64 in `.montage`. Bundles without `moduleinfo.json` now need the out-of-process probe. Add Steinberg's MIT notice to the third-party credits, and do not use the VST logo without following Steinberg's trademark rules ([VST 3 Licensing](https://steinbergmedia.github.io/vst3_dev_portal/pages/VST+3+Licensing/Index.html)).

**#10 Plugin editor windows (L).** Lacking embedded plugin UIs is a stated limitation of Shotcut's plugin support ([Shotcut forum](https://forum.shotcut.org/t/external-plugin-support-openfx-vst2-and-lv2/51741)), and Kdenlive's plugin backend is blocked on "state saving and UI" ([Kdenlive roadmap](https://kdenlive.org/roadmap/)). Each format embeds differently. CLAP calls `is_api_supported`, `create`, scale and size, then `set_parent` with a native handle and `show`; Wayland embedding is unsupported, so CLAP editors float there ([clap gui.h](https://github.com/free-audio/clap/blob/main/include/clap/ext/gui.h)). VST3 passes an HWND, NSView or XEmbed window to `IPlugView::attached` and needs an `IRunLoop` on Linux ([iplugview.h](https://github.com/steinbergmedia/vst3_pluginterfaces/blob/master/gui/iplugview.h)). LV2 UIs embed through suil (ISC), and AUv3 editors come from AVFAudio's view controller. On the Qt side, each editor sits in a container widget whose `winId()` is the parent handle ([Qt window embedding](https://doc.qt.io/qt-6.10/qtdoc-demos-windowembedding-example.html)), with sizes multiplied by `devicePixelRatio()` on Windows and X11 but not on Cocoa. On Linux, editors run under XWayland, and CLAP fd and timer callbacks are driven from `QSocketNotifier` and `QTimer`. GUI gesture begin and end events become keyframes merged into one undo step, and a generic slider panel stays as the fallback.

**#11 Track and bus effect chains, delay compensation and offline render (L).** Today plugins exist only per clip, and tracks carry only a fader and pan. Fairlight's real-time effects on up to 2,000 tracks set the expectation ([Blackmagic](https://www.blackmagicdesign.com/products/davinciresolve)), and Kdenlive lists submix buses as a gap ([Kdenlive roadmap](https://kdenlive.org/roadmap/)). The model needs effect stacks on tracks, submix buses with track-to-bus-to-master routing, and plugin delay compensation that sums each chain's reported latency and delays shorter paths to match. Export pre-rolls by the latency, discards the first samples, and renders each plugin's tail past the out point. Before export, plugins switch to offline mode by deactivating, reconfiguring and reactivating them: CLAP uses `render.set(OFFLINE)` ([clap render.h](https://github.com/free-audio/clap/blob/main/include/clap/ext/render.h)) and VST3 `setupProcessing(kOffline)`, and both formats allow latency changes only across that cycle ([clap latency.h](https://github.com/free-audio/clap/blob/main/include/clap/ext/latency.h)). Export uses its own plugin instances, separate from playback, and `montage-cli` always renders offline.

**#12 Audio Unit hosting on macOS (M).** AudioToolbox handles AUv2 (`AudioComponentInstanceNew`) and AVFAudio's `AUAudioUnit` handles AUv3. Montage already lists AUs from the system component registry (`AudioComponentFindNext`) without opening them, and Apple's `AVAudioUnitComponentManager` exposes the same registry "without opening them" while adding tag search for the browser ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/avfaudio/avaudiounitcomponentmanager)). Montage should request `kAudioComponentInstantiation_LoadOutOfProcess` ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/audiotoolbox/audiocomponentinstantiationoptions/loadoutofprocess)): since macOS 11 the system can host AUs in `AUHostingServiceXPC`, isolating crashes at no cost to Montage, though Apple warns hosts not to assume callbacks arrive on the main thread ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/audiotoolbox/debugging-out-of-process-audio-units-on-apple-silicon)). Two follow-ups remain: record a Logic-style "failed validation" status so the UI can explain hidden plugins, and confirm the hardened-runtime entitlement needed to load third-party code, which the research did not source.

**#17 LV2 hosting, Linux first (M).** Montage should link a system lilv (0.28 or later) and suil through pkg-config rather than vendoring them, because lilv builds with Meson ([lilv meson.build](https://gitlab.com/lv2/lilv/-/blob/main/meson.build)). Both carry ISC-style licences ([lilv COPYING](https://gitlab.com/lv2/lilv/-/blob/main/COPYING); [suil COPYING](https://gitlab.com/lv2/suil/-/blob/main/COPYING)), and lilv 0.28.0 (June 2026) fixed state-saving data-loss bugs, so it is the minimum for project state ([lilv NEWS](https://gitlab.com/lv2/lilv/-/blob/main/NEWS)). `LV2_PATH` and the platform defaults come from the LV2 filesystem standard ([LV2 FHS](https://lv2plug.in/pages/filesystem-hierarchy-standard.html)). The serd, sord, sratom and zix licences are believed to be ISC but were not verified, so check them before shipping. Linux is the natural beachhead: Premiere and FCP have no Linux builds, and Resolve's free Linux tier lacks H.264 ([Hacker News](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47815118)).

### Picture: masks, colour, tracking and retiming

**#9 Shape masks and HSL qualifier (M).** Power windows and qualifiers are core Resolve grading tools ([Blackmagic](https://www.blackmagicdesign.com/products/davinciresolve)), and Premiere rebuilt its shape masks in 26.0 ([ProVideo Coalition](https://www.provideocoalition.com/new-ai-and-masking-tools-in-premiere-plus-major-upgrade-to-after-e%ef%ac%80ects/)). Montage needs a mask object on any clip or effect, with rectangle, ellipse and Bézier pen shapes; feather, expansion, opacity and invert controls; add and subtract modes; and keyframeable vertices. It rasterises with `QPainterPath` into a float alpha matte using Qt's `Format_RGBA32FPx4` ([Qt QImage](https://doc.qt.io/qt-6/qimage.html)). An HSL key with softness produces the same matte type for secondary correction, and tracking (#16) and SAM 2 (#20) later drive this same mask system.

**#13 OCIO colour management with ACES 2.0 and HDR deliverables (L).** OpenColorIO (BSD-3) is the de facto standard. Its current 2.5.x line (2.5.2 shipped in May 2026) requires C++17, ships built-in ACES 2.0 configs such as `ocio://studio-config-v4.0.0_aces-v2.0_ocio-v2.5`, and generates shaders for GLSL, HLSL, MSL and Vulkan GLSL 4.6 ([OCIO 2.5 notes](https://opencolorio.readthedocs.io/en/latest/releases/ocio_2_5.html); [OCIO TAC](https://tac.aswf.io/meetings/2026-03-18/OCIO_TAC_Update-March_026.pdf)). The leaders set the bar: Premiere 25.0 converts log and raw "from nearly every camera" into an ACEScct-based working space ([CineD](https://www.cined.com/adobe-premiere-pro-and-after-effects-25-in-beta-new-color-management-improved-properties-panel-enhanced-3d-workflows-and-more/)), and Resolve 20 added ACES 2.0 with OCIO 2.4.2 ([Newsshooter](https://www.newsshooter.com/2025/05/27/davinci-resolve-20-final-release/)). Montage's pipeline derives each clip's input space from FFmpeg colour tags, with a camera-log override, and composites in scene-linear (ACEScg, or linear Rec.709 in a "video" mode). The viewer applies OCIO display and view transforms; export applies output transforms and writes PQ/HLG tags and HDR10 metadata; and scopes are computed after the chosen transform, with a nits scale for HDR. OCIO's CPU processor serves the current compositor and the scopes now, so viewer and scopes match, and GPU shaders follow with #15. Replacing the `.cube` loader with OCIO `FileTransform` adds 3DL and CLF support for free. OCIO 2.6.0 was planned for 30 September 2026; whether it shipped is unconfirmed.

**#16 Tracking and stabilisation (L).** OpenCV (Apache-2.0) offers the CSRT and KCF trackers in opencv_contrib and the Nano and Vit DNN trackers in the main `video` module ([OpenCV tracking](https://docs.opencv.org/4.x/d9/df8/group__tracking.html)), and tracker output becomes transform or mask keyframes. There is no turnkey open planar tracker, so corner-pin tracking comes from KLT optical flow plus RANSAC `findHomography`, or from libmv's region tracker (MIT, Blender's tracker) ([libmv](https://projects.blender.org/blender/blender/src/branch/main/intern/libmv/libmv/tracking/track_region.h)). For stabilisation, Montage should link vid.stab directly: it was relicensed to **LGPL-2.1-or-later** precisely so applications can use it without the GPL, even though FFmpeg's configure still gates it behind `--enable-gpl` ([vid.stab](https://github.com/georgmartius/vid.stab)). Per-frame transforms are stored in the project and applied with zoom-to-fill, avoiding FFmpeg's two-pass file workflow. Gyroflow (GPL-3.0) is an optional gyro-data path ([Gyroflow](https://github.com/gyroflow/gyroflow)).

**#18 Speed ramps and optical-flow retiming (L).** Resolve's Speed Warp is Studio-only ([Blackmagic Studio](https://www.blackmagicdesign.com/products/davinciresolve/studio)), and FCP has shipped Smooth Slo-Mo since 10.8 ([Apple release notes](https://support.apple.com/en-us/102825)); Montage today has only constant speed. A keyframed speed curve, integrated into a frame-accurate source-time map, gives ramps, with three interpolation modes: nearest frame, frame blending and optical flow. Optical flow uses RIFE through rife-ncnn-vulkan (MIT) on ncnn (BSD-3), or Practical-RIFE (MIT) exported to ONNX ([rife-ncnn-vulkan](https://github.com/nihui/rife-ncnn-vulkan); [ncnn](https://github.com/Tencent/ncnn/blob/master/LICENSE.txt)), with generated frames cached per clip and speed and FFmpeg's LGPL `minterpolate` as the CPU fallback ([vf_minterpolate.c](https://github.com/FFmpeg/FFmpeg/blob/master/libavfilter/vf_minterpolate.c)). The RIFE model-weight licence needs separate verification, because it is not in the repositories, and the LGPL build should avoid `librubberband` for pitch-preserving audio, because FFmpeg lists it as GPL ([FFmpeg configure](https://github.com/FFmpeg/FFmpeg/blob/master/configure)).

**#20 AI object masks with SAM 2 (XL).** SAM 2's code and checkpoints are Apache-2.0; its models run from Tiny (38.9M parameters) to Large (224.4M) and use streaming memory for video ([SAM 2](https://github.com/facebookresearch/sam2)). Kdenlive 25.04 already ships a local SAM 2 masking plugin through a Python virtual environment and warns about its high memory use ([Kdenlive 25.04](https://kdenlive.org/news/releases/25.04.0/)). Montage should run it natively through ONNX Runtime. The user clicks or draws a box on a keyframe, and Montage encodes and decodes that frame and propagates the mask across frames, which needs a custom ONNX export because of SAM 2's memory-attention state. Per-frame alpha mattes are cached and fed into the #9 mask system, with optional edge refinement from BiRefNet (MIT) ([BiRefNet](https://github.com/ZhengPeng7/BiRefNet)). Budget for the hardware cost, since Resolve gates its AI at 16 GB of VRAM on PC.

### Multicam and interchange

**#14 Multicam clips with speaker auto-switch (L).** A multicam source becomes a nested sequence of angles, synced by timecode, in points or Montage's existing waveform cross-correlation. Timeline segments carry an active angle, and every angle switch is an undoable edit. The viewer shows a 2×2, 3×3 or 4×4 grid decoded from proxies, which needs #2's decoder cap. Editors switch live with number keys during playback, as in Kdenlive ([Kdenlive features](https://kdenlive.org/features/)); audio either follows the video angle or stays fixed; and a "flatten" command converts the result to normal clips. Resolve 21.1 now maps keyboard shortcuts to up to 25 angles ([CineD](https://www.cined.com/davinci-resolve-21-1-released-ai-assistant-integration-via-mcp-individual-hdr-trims-and-python-scripting-moves-to-studio/)). Auto-switching reuses #4's speaker labels: Avid builds its Auto Cut by mapping transcript speakers to angles, with settings for timing offset and minimum speech duration ([RedShark](https://www.redsharknews.com/media-composer-2026-8-features)), and Resolve shipped the same idea as SmartSwitch in May 2025, about 15 months earlier. ProVideo Coalition warns the result will not catch reactions from people who are not speaking ([ProVideo Coalition](https://www.provideocoalition.com/avid-media-composer-2026-8-is-now-available/)), so Montage should present it as a first pass for the editor to refine.

**#19 Interchange round trip (L).** The hand-written OTIO JSON in `src/core/Interchange.cpp` should give way to the OpenTimelineIO C++ core (Apache-2.0) for both import and export ([OTIO](https://github.com/AcademySoftwareFoundation/OpenTimelineIO)). Compound clips map to nested Stacks, speed to `LinearTimeWarp`, markers to `Marker`, and effects to metadata, as Kdenlive's C++ rewrite does ([Kdenlive 25.04](https://kdenlive.org/news/releases/25.04.0/)). CMX 3600 EDL import joins the existing export. FCP7 XML and FCPXML readers and writers use `QXmlStreamReader` and `QXmlStreamWriter`, with the Python OpenTimelineIO-Plugins adapters as behavioural references ([otio-fcpx-xml-adapter](https://pypi.org/project/otio-fcpx-xml-adapter)); FCP is now on FCPXML 1.14 ([Apple release notes](https://support.apple.com/en-us/102825)). The timing favours OTIO. Premiere's support has sat in beta for "over 2 years" ([Adobe Community](https://community.adobe.com/questions-729/opentimelineio-otio-export-option-missing-in-premiere-pro-s-export-menu-1418570)) while Avid shipped an OTIO 2.0 adapter in 2026.8, so solid OTIO plus FCPXML would put Montage ahead of Premiere on open interchange. AAF waits for Phase 3, because LibAAF (GPL-2.0-or-later) only reads AAF and is audio-oriented ([LibAAF](https://github.com/agfline/LibAAF)), and export would need pyaaf2 (MIT, Python) or the custom-licensed AAF SDK.

## Leaders scan out of process, block first and cache by file identity

Ardour is the only market-grade host whose scanner can be read line by line, and it sets the pattern ([plugin_manager.cc](https://github.com/Ardour/ardour/blob/master/libs/ardour/plugin_manager.cc)). It runs a separate scanner executable per format, once per bundle, under a configurable timeout. It blocks first: it calls `vst3_blacklist(module_path)` before launching the scanner and clears the entry only after the scanner's cache file parses, so a crash or hang leaves the plugin blocked automatically. It keeps one cache file per module, named by the SHA-1 of its path and valid while the plugin's mtime is older than the cache file's, and a `cache_only` mode lists plugins at launch without scanning. Its VST3 scanner also instantiates the component to record input, output, auxiliary and MIDI bus counts, which `moduleinfo.json` does not carry ([vst3_scan.cc](https://github.com/Ardour/ardour/blob/master/libs/ardour/vst3_scan.cc)).

Apple adds validation to the same pattern. Logic runs every Audio Unit through Apple's validator, excludes failures "to prevent possible problems or crashes", labels them "failed validation" or "crashed validation", and offers "Reset & Rescan Selection" and a full AU reset in its Plug-in Manager ([Apple Support](https://support.apple.com/en-ng/122179)); from macOS 11 the system itself can load AUs out of process ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/audiotoolbox/debugging-out-of-process-audio-units-on-apple-silicon)). Bitwig goes furthest by isolating plugins while they run: five hosting modes range from "Within Bitwig" (in the engine, where one crash takes all audio down) to "Individually", with a shared sandbox as the default, and a crashed plugin shows "Reload Plug-in" in its UI ([Bitwig user guide](https://bitwig.com/userguide/latest/vst_plug-in_handling_and_options)).

The NLE vendors document far less, and their records show the cost. Premiere exposes only an Audio Plug-in Manager with scan, rescan and per-plugin enable switches ([ProVideo Coalition](https://www.provideocoalition.com/tool-tip-tuesday-for-adobe-premiere-pro-tame-your-audio-effects/)), and a 2026 bug report says the 26.5 beta "does not scan any vst3 or au audio plug-ins on macos" ([Adobe Community](https://community.adobe.com/questions-734/premiere-beta-26-5-27-0-does-not-scan-any-vst3-or-au-audio-plug-ins-on-macos-1644421)). Resolve 17.4.2 had to fix "rescanning on startup with crashed VST3 plugins" ([Newsshooter](https://www.newsshooter.com/2021/11/18/blackmagic-davinci-resolve-17-4-2/)), and users still report scanner loops that never blocklist the culprit, though these are anecdotal forum reports ([Blackmagic Forum](https://forum.blackmagicdesign.com/viewtopic.php?p=772583)). Ableton users report Live 12 freezing at start-up during plugin scans ([Ableton forum](https://forum.ableton.com/viewtopic.php?p=1827150)). None of these three publishes its internal scanner design, so whether they scan out of process is unknown.

| Host | Scan isolation | Crashing or hanging plugin | Cache | User controls | Runtime isolation |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Ardour | Scanner process per format, per bundle, with timeout | Blocked before probing; unblocked only on success | Per-module file; path SHA-1 + mtime | Cancel one/all; clear block list; cache-only start-up | In process |
| Logic / FCP | Apple AU validation | Excluded as "failed" or "crashed validation" | Not documented | Reset & Rescan Selection; full AU reset | macOS 11+ out-of-process AUs |
| Bitwig | Not documented | Crash notice and Reload | Not documented | Hosting mode per plugin | Five sandbox modes |
| REAPER | Not documented | Stays blocked even after "Clear cache/re-scan" | Plain-text ini | Clear cache and re-scan | Per-plugin 32-bit bridge |
| Premiere | Not documented | Not documented | Not documented | Scan, rescan existing, enable checkboxes | Not documented |
| Resolve | Not documented | Users report loops without blocking | Not documented | Not documented | Not documented |
| Shotcut 26.6 | In process | Safe mode skips plugins after an early crash | Not documented | None | None |
| Montage today | `montage-plugin-probe` per file, serial, 15 s timeout | Blocked with reason until the file changes or the user retries | One JSON file; path + size + newest mtime | API only (`rescanBlocked`, `unblock`, search paths) | CLAP in process |

The REAPER row comes from vendor support pages ([Mixwave](https://support.mixwave.com/doc/reaper-troubleshooting)) and the Shotcut row from its 26.6 announcement ([Shotcut forum](https://forum.shotcut.org/t/external-plugin-support-openfx-vst2-and-lv2/51741)). Montage's scanner already matches the best documented practice in structure. It never runs plugin code in the editor during a scan, it reads VST3 `moduleinfo.json` and LV2 manifests without executing anything, which the VST3 spec explicitly allows ([ModuleInfo-JSON](https://steinbergmedia.github.io/vst3_dev_portal/pages/Technical+Documentation/VST+Module+Architecture/ModuleInfo-JSON.html)), and it blocklists crashes, non-zero exits, timeouts and unparsable output.

Five hardening steps remain, all small enough to fold into #3 and #6. Probes should run in a bounded parallel `QProcess` pool, because today they run serially and a large plugin folder takes a long time. Each result should be committed as it arrives, because the cache is currently written only once the whole scan finishes, so quitting mid-scan loses all progress. The cache key should add host CPU architecture and a scanner schema version, so an x64-to-arm64 migration cannot reuse stale entries. Channel layout and latency should be re-read when a plugin is inserted on a clip or track, since CLAP reports latency only once activated ([clap latency.h](https://github.com/free-audio/clap/blob/main/include/clap/ext/latency.h)) and Ardour reads VST3 bus counts only from a live instance. And CI should carry an in-tree MIT gain plugin plus deliberately crashing and hanging plugins, so the timeout and blocklist stay tested on all three operating systems; Premiere's 2026 regression shows how easily scanning breaks. Runtime crash isolation in the Bitwig style needs shared-memory audio, parameter IPC and editor windows reparented across processes, so it belongs in Phase 3; until then, item #1's plugin safe mode and the free AU isolation on macOS cover the worst case.

## Conclusion

The leaders' 2026 AI race converged on one structure: the transcript has become the index of an edit. Avid's multicam cut, Resolve's IntelliScript and ADR cueing, FCP's Transcript Search and Premiere's Creative Agent all read the same word-timed, speaker-labelled text, which makes a single local whisper.cpp and sherpa-onnx integration the highest-leverage investment Montage can make, and one an open-source editor can ship with no credits, no cloud and no new GPL obligations. The licence landscape flipped in the same period: VST3 went MIT, vid.stab went LGPL and SAM 2 is Apache-2.0, so almost the whole professional layer is now buildable from permissive parts, and the only GPL pieces in the plan are the x264/x265 encoders Montage already ships.

Meanwhile, the incumbents are monetising openness. Resolve moved Python scripting and its new MCP server to Studio, and Avid moved bin locking on third-party storage to Enterprise; those are exactly the seams an open project can exploit, and Montage's pure-function edit operations and `montage-cli` make an open automation and MCP API cheap, which is why it heads the Phase 3 list. None of this matters if Montage loses work. The evidence is consistent that editors switch tools over trust, not features, which is why the cheapest item on the list, crash-safe history, comes first.

## Phase 2 roadmap: twenty items in priority order

Effort is in person-weeks for one experienced contributor: S ≤ 2, M 2–6, L 6–12, XL > 12. At the range midpoints the plan totals roughly 2.5–3 person-years, and the XL items (#15, #20) should start early on a parallel track because of their length.

1. [ ] **Crash-safe project history and recovery**: debounced live save after each edit, rolling timestamped autosave snapshots, unclean-exit detection with a recovery dialog, and plugin safe mode after a crash. **Effort: S**
2. [ ] **Hardware decode and encode**: FFmpeg hwaccel decode (VideoToolbox, D3D11VA/D3D12VA, VAAPI, NVDEC, Vulkan) with software fallback and a decoder cap, plus hardware encoder presets. **Effort: M**
3. [ ] **Audio plugin manager UI**: Preferences › Audio Plug-ins with enable switches, scan new, rescan selected, full rescan including blocked, blocked list with reasons and retry, search folders, scan log, and a parallel, incremental, architecture-aware background scan. **Effort: S**
4. [ ] **Transcription engine**: in-process whisper.cpp with VAD and word timestamps, a model download manager, and sherpa-onnx speaker labels, stored as per-media transcripts. **Effort: M**
