# Phase 8 roadmap (October 2026)

A seventh gap analysis, made as Phase 7 drew to a close. It compares Montage with Premiere 26.3 and 26.5 (to 26.5.2), DaVinci Resolve 21.1 and 21.1.1, Final Cut Pro 12.3 and 12.4 and Media Composer 2026.8, with what Avid and Blackmagic showed at IBC 2026. It also looks at Baselight v7, Nuke 17.0 and 17.1, Pro Tools 2026.4.1, Nuendo, Descript (to its 12 October changelog), CapCut and Kdenlive 26.08, and at open tools such as DCP-o-matic, IMF Tool and untrunc. Every candidate was checked against `README.md` and the `PROGRESS.md` checklists and their "Not done" notes. These are already there and are not proposed again: Convert Voice (speech-to-speech), What's Heard (sound search), Relight, ProRes RAW and CinemaDNG, watch folders, Paper Edit, Compare with Sequence with change lists and re-conform, scene-cut detection, AAF export with picture, AS-11 and RDD 9 MXF, IMF App 2E with HTJ2K, DCPs checked with ClairMeta, interlaced exports and field order in Interpret Footage, Lens Correction from lensfun, synthetic focus, dialogue match and convolution reverb, LTC, remote review over SRT, gyro stabilisation, film restoration, expressions, lip sync, Face Shape and Look at Lens, Sky Replacement, monitor calibration, Film Look, Deflicker, Spectral Repair, Magnify, Channel Blur, OGraf and Lottie, and the MCP agent link. Dolby Vision, per-bin locking, direct publishing and generated media stay out.

Ranking is value to a professional editor or colourist × feasibility in C++/Qt/FFmpeg with optional local ONNX models. Nothing below needs a paid SDK, an account, a cloud API or an API key. Every dependency and model is GPL-3.0-compatible unless **flagged**. Everything can be built and tested in the Linux cloud container except where a row says otherwise. Sizes: S ≤ 2, M 2–6, L 6–12 person-weeks.

## Earlier leftovers carried forward

- **GPU compositor** (Phase 6 #1): three parts are done. Still open: OCIO on the GPU, depth, people and object masks, Refine Edges, the HDR Palette, the remaining effects, YUV frames uploaded as decoded, decoding into GPU memory, exports on the GPU and a speed check on a real GPU. Related: frames are 16-bit, so an OpenEXR's values above 1 still clip (Phase 7 #9). A half-float frame path belongs with this work.
- **Blackmagic RAW** (Phase 6 #2) is still blocked on the SDK's terms.
- **Capture** (Phase 6 #13): DeckLink, AJA and NDI input, deck control and batch capture remain, and none of them can be tested here except 9-pin deck control (against a pseudo-terminal; see Lower priority). **MV-HEVC out** (Phase 6 #15) now has a route that can be tested here: #15 below.
- **Camera motion data** (Phase 7 #6): Sony rtmd, DJI, Canon, Blackmagic RAW and Insta360's own trailer, through telemetry-parser (MIT or Apache-2.0) behind a small C ABI as planned. Lens distortion in the gyro warp too. It needs sample files from each camera.
- Small Phase 7 remainders worth doing alongside: two takes side by side from a script line and script revision colours (#1); true-stereo impulse responses and a room tail that rings past the clip's end (#2); Bandit v2's other languages once their checkpoints are released (#3); deep EXR and lossy HTJ2K (#9); the monitor LUT on the GPU (#11); a face mesh steadied over time and lip sync on more than the largest face (#13, #15); expressions carried through AAF and OTIO (#14). Dirt that sits on two frames and slanting scratches (#7) go into #12 below. Steadying depth, focus that follows a tracked object (#8) and a steady sky matte (#12) go into #7 below.
- Small Phase 6 remainders: the dialogue-gated loudness range (LRA); roll-up, paint-on and service-2 708 captions; Tangent controls for Curves, the Colour Warper and the HDR Palette; split screen across several stills. Automatic trims (Phase 6 #5) are #8 below.
- **Prompted sound isolation** still has no model with usable terms. SAM Audio now comes in a 500 M small size, but it is under Meta's own SAM License, which is not GPL-compatible, and its download is gated (**flag**). **Temporally consistent inpainting** is in the same position: MiniMax-Remover's weights are CC BY-NC 4.0, and ProPainter is non-commercial.

| 1 | Conform check against the offline reference: lay the editor's reference movie against an imported timeline. Flag every shot that differs: a wrong take, a slip of some frames, a missing shot, or a different reposition. Solve each shot's reposition (scale, rotation, position, keyed when it moves) from the reference | Baselight v7 (Transform Matching), Resolve (offline reference clips with a Difference wipe) | S–M | In-house, on what exists. Sequence › Load Offline Reference attaches a movie to a sequence by timecode, or by its first frame. It plays in the Source monitor in step with the Program, as a wipe, side by side or as a difference. The check renders both at 1/4 size and normalises each channel, so a grade does not count. Burn-ins are masked: regions that stay fixed across cuts, or text PP-OCR reads. Each shot is compared by normalised cross-correlation after a similarity fit. A shot that matches only at another offset (±2 s) is reported as slipped by that many frames. A shot that matches at no offset is compared with the other takes of its scene through Find Similar Shots' CLIP index, which names the right take. The reposition comes from the existing Shi–Tomasi corners, Lucas–Kanade and a RANSAC similarity fit between source and reference, sampled every 12 frames and keyed only where it changes. Results are listed and marked like Compare with Sequence's, each with Apply Fix. MCP `montage_conform_check`. Test: a three-shot reference with a 1.12× push, a 1.5° turn and a 40 px move on one shot, another shot slipped 3 frames, a third swapped for its other take, and a burnt-in timecode. All three are found, the move is within 0.5 px and 0.1°, and an identical conform flags nothing |
| 2 | Dialogue alignment: fit an ADR take, a pickup or a converted voice to the guide track's timing, keeping its pitch (as VocAlign does), or line up several microphones of one take with the boom by shifting alone | Nuendo and Cubase (Audio Alignment); Pro Tools with Synchro Arts VocAlign or Revoice Pro (paid plug-ins) | S–M | In-house. Both sides become 10 ms log-mel frames with deltas, gated to speech (the detector Dialog LKFS uses). Dynamic time warping runs in a Sakoe–Chiba band with slope limits. Its path is smoothed into a monotone warp with anchors at syllable onsets, and an Alignment Precision setting thins them. WSOLA, which keep-pitch conforms already use, applies the warp. It is kept as a source-audio effect (`dialogue_align`, a warp table in source seconds), cached like Noise Reduction, undoable and removable. Prefer Shifting uses the waveform sync's cross-correlation at sub-sample precision, for multi-mic takes. The ADR panel gets Align to Guide on each take, and Convert Voice and Regenerate Words can use it. MCP `montage_align_dialogue`. Test: speech (jfk.wav and Kokoro lines) warped by a known piecewise curve of ±20 % comes back with its onsets within 10 ms and the guide's length. A second mic, polarity-flipped and 3.2 ms late, is shifted to within a sample with no stretch |
| 3 | DCP for festivals and distribution: SMPTE subtitles and closed captions from a caption track, encrypted packages with KDMs for named cinema servers (and DKDMs to keep), 4K, and stereoscopic 3D | DCP-o-matic (free, GPL); easyDCP (paid; Resolve Studio's DCP route goes through easyDCP or Kakadu) | M | Extends render/DcpMxf and the DCP exporter. Subtitles: SMPTE ST 428-7 XML (timed text with an embedded font, the caption's position and italics), wrapped as a timed-text track file (ST 429-5) and placed in the CPL as MainSubtitle or MainClosedCaption, as Bv2.1 asks. Burning in stays an option. Encryption: per-frame AES-128-CBC with MIC keys (ST 429-6), the content keys kept in the project's keystore. KDMs (ST 430-1) go to one or more server leaf certificates with a validity window, signed by a certificate chain Montage makes once per user. Both use OpenSSL 3 (Apache-2.0), with an XML-DSig enveloped signature over Exclusive C14N (xmlsec, MIT). 4K: Flat 3996 × 2160 and Scope 4096 × 1716 in the DCI 4K profile. 3D: the stereo sequence's eyes interleaved at twice the rate (ST 429-10). Test: ClairMeta (BSD-3-Clause) passes plain and encrypted packages, the encrypted one checked with the KDM and the target's private key. asdcplib decrypts a frame back to Montage's codestream. The subtitles pass ClairMeta's subtitle inspection and read back through Montage's own reader |
| 4 | Recover interrupted recordings: rebuild a camera or recorder file whose recording stopped without writing its index (a flat battery or a pulled card) from a good clip from the same camera; also MXF without its footer, and WAV or BWF files with wrong sizes | Descript (Fix file issue, October 2026), untrunc, Grau Video Repair and other paid tools; no NLE | S–M | In-house. untrunc is GPL-2.0 with no "or later" shown, so none of its code is used. The reference clip gives the sample descriptions (avcC or hvcC, ProRes, PCM or AAC) and the camera's interleave (from its stsc and stsz: so many video samples, then an audio chunk of a set size). The damaged mdat is walked: H.264 and HEVC samples by their NAL length prefixes and valid NAL headers, ProRes by its `icpf` frame header and size, PCM by the reference's chunk size, AAC by trial decoding with FFmpeg. A new moov (stts, stsz, stco, stss) is written after it. Start timecode comes from a sidecar where the camera writes one, else zero, and the item is labelled as recovered. MXF is re-indexed by its essence KLV keys, with a closed header carrying the duration and a footer with an index table. A WAV's RIFF and data sizes are set from the file's length (RF64 past 4 GiB), keeping bext and iXML. It is offered as Media bin › Repair Media, and when Offload Card finds a copy that will not open. Test: FFmpeg-made H.264 with AAC in MOV, HEVC with PCM in MP4 and ProRes with PCM in MOV, cut at 70 % with the moov gone. Every whole frame decodes identical to the original, and the sound is sample-exact to the last whole chunk. An MXF without its footer and a WAV with zero sizes open after repair |
| 5 | IMF versions: subtitle and caption tracks (IMSC 1.1), several sound languages, supplemental packages that carry only what changed since the original, and IMF packages opened as sequences | Resolve 21 and 21.1 (supplemental IMF), IMF Tool (open source), Clipster and Transkoder (paid) | M | Extends render/Imf. | 6 | Pulldown and frame-rate conversion for delivery: remove 2:3 pulldown (including cadence broken by old edits), add it on export (23.976p to 29.97i), speed a programme up or down for PAL (24 to 25, pitch kept or not), and convert standards with motion compensation (25 ↔ 29.97, 50 ↔ 59.94) | Resolve (Remove 3:2 Pulldown in Clip Attributes; pulldown added when rendering 23.976 to 29.97), Media Composer (film projects); paid standards converters | S–M | In-house. The cadence is found from field differences (each frame's top and bottom fields against its neighbours'), stretch by stretch, so a break at an old edit starts it again. Interpret Footage gets Remove Pulldown (Auto, or starting on A to D). Export adds 2:3 pulldown with field order on 29.97i deliveries (AS-11 and RDD 9 take it) and 2:2:2:4 for 59.94p. A 25→29.97 conversion has no blended frame at the cuts |
| 7 | Steady depth and focus that follows: depth that does not shimmer on video, for Lens Blur (Depth), Depth Fog, Depth Map, Sky Replacement and Relight; focus that follows a tracked point, object or face; a sky matte held steady through a shot | Resolve 21 (CineFocus, Depth Map), Final Cut 12.4 (Cinematic focus points), Baselight v7 (Depth Map, Bokeh, Haze) | M | Video Depth Anything Small (Apache-2.0, 28.4 M parameters), exported to ONNX here and hosted pinned like Depth Anything V2. | 8 | Automatic trims from the HDR master: a starting SDR trim (and a lower-peak HDR trim) for each shot from its own light levels, evened out across a scene, for the colourist to adjust; master and target side by side | Dolby Vision analysis in Resolve and Baselight (proprietary), Resolve 21.1 (individual MultiMaster trims) | S–M | In-house, on the HDR trim passes and Analyse HDR Light Levels, which already measures scene by scene. Each shot's log-average, 1st and 99.9th percentiles and peak (in nits) set its trim. Exposure brings mid-grey to where Montage's HDR-to-SDR mapping puts BT.2408's 203-nit reference white. The highlight roll-off knee comes from the shot's peak, there is a lift for crushed shadows, and saturation compensates for the roll-off. Shots in one scene (the analysis's scene breaks, or a colour group) are pulled towards their median, so a cut does not jump. Only shots with no trims of their own are filled, in one undo step (Analyse Trims). The Program monitor's split view shows master and target at once (Phase 6's leftover). Test: synthetic night, daylight, specular sea and sunset shots each reach SDR with mid-grey within 2 % of target and under 0.5 % of pixels clipped. Neighbouring shots in a scene end within 0.3 stops of each other |
| 9 | Lens calibration: make a lens profile from a checkerboard clip, or from lines that should be straight, anamorphics included; ST maps for VFX | Resolve 21 (Fusion Lens Distort with checkerboard calibration), Resolve 21.1 (calibration from lines drawn on the viewer), Nuke (LensDistortion) | S–M | In-house (Montage uses no OpenCV). The result is saved as lensfun XML in the user's lens folder, so Lens Correction offers it like any other profile. VFX pulls can carry undistort and redistort ST maps (32-bit EXR, Nuke's convention) with the overscan size. Test: a board rendered through known poly5 terms, a centre offset and a 1.33 squeeze at eight poses. Reprojection is under 0.1 px and the terms are within 1 %. Plumb lines come out straight within 0.2 px, and the ST map warps a grid exactly as Lens Correction does |

| 12 | Film scans: negative inversion from the film base, printer lights in points, restoration of faded dyes, steadying on the perforations, and the dirt and scratches Restoration still misses | Resolve (printer points on Offset; dirt, dust and deflicker tools), Baselight (printer lights), DIAMANT-Film, MTI DRS Nova and PFClean (paid) | M | In-house.
