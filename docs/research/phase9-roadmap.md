# Phase 9 roadmap (October 2026)

An eighth gap analysis, made as Phase 8 drew to a close. It compares Montage with Premiere 26.3 and 26.5 (to 26.5.2) and After Effects 26.5 with its beta, DaVinci Resolve 21.1 and 21.1.1 (no 21.2 has been announced), Final Cut Pro 12.3 and 12.4 and Media Composer 2026.8. Adobe MAX is on 10–12 November, after this analysis. It also looks at Baselight v7, Nuke 17.1, Pro Tools 2026.4.1 and its mid-year update, Nuendo 15, Descript (to its 13 October roundup), CapCut desktop 9.6, Kdenlive 26.08.2 and Shotcut 26.9 (Olive has had no release since 2024), and at open tools: FFmpeg 8.1, OpenColorIO 2.5, OpenTimelineIO 0.18, OpenFX 1.5, OpenAPV and Open Media Transport. Every candidate was checked against `README.md` and the `PROGRESS.md` checklists and their "Not done" notes. These are already there and are not proposed again. From Phase 8: the conform check against an offline reference, dialogue alignment, DCPs with subtitles, encryption and KDMs, recovering interrupted recordings, IMF versions and supplementals, pulldown and delivery at another frame rate, steady video depth and Follow Focus, automatic trims from the HDR master, lens calibration and ST maps, IAMF, HDR stills with gain maps, film scans, words from the footage, the Avid OP-Atom hand-off and MV-HEVC out. From earlier: Auto Colour, Auto Duck Music, Auto Mix's dialogue levelling, De-Esser, De-Click, De-Hum, De-Reverb, Spectral Repair, mic bleed removal, speaker detection, Separate Stems and the dialogue, music and effects split, track folders, the photosensitive flash check, Lens Flare, Motion Deblur, AI upscaling, Face Refinement, jump-cut morphs, review drawings and replies, OpenFX and audio plugin hosting, NDI and DeckLink reference output, Lottie and OGraf, VR180, and the MCP agent link. Dolby Vision, per-bin locking, direct publishing and generated media stay out.

Ranking is value to a professional editor or colourist × feasibility in C++/Qt/FFmpeg with optional local ONNX and GGUF models. Nothing below needs a paid SDK, an account, a cloud API or an API key. Every dependency and model is GPL-3.0-compatible unless **flagged**. Everything can be built and tested in the Linux cloud container except where a row says otherwise. Sizes: S ≤ 2, M 2–6, L 6–12 person-weeks.

## Earlier leftovers carried forward

- **GPU compositor** (Phase 6 #1): three parts are done. Still open: OCIO on the GPU, depth, people and object masks, Refine Edges, the HDR Palette, the remaining effects, YUV frames uploaded as decoded, decoding into GPU memory, exports on the GPU, Vulkan and a speed check on a real GPU. Frames are still 16-bit, so an OpenEXR's values above 1 clip (Phase 7 #9). That is now #2 below.
- **Blackmagic RAW** (Phase 6 #2) is still blocked on the SDK's terms.
- **Capture** (Phase 6 #13): DeckLink, AJA and NDI input, deck control and batch capture remain. Only 9-pin deck control can be tested here (against a pseudo-terminal; see Lower priority). Network input without a proprietary runtime is #15 below.
- **Camera motion data** (Phase 7 #6): Sony rtmd, DJI, Canon, Blackmagic RAW and Insta360's own trailer, through telemetry-parser (MIT or Apache-2.0) behind a small C ABI as planned, and lens distortion in the gyro warp. It needs sample files from each camera.
- **Phase 8 remainders worth doing alongside:** the offline reference playing in step in the Source monitor with a wipe or difference view, and finding a take by a picture index over the whole project (#1); Regenerate Words aligning its result by itself (#2); Interop DCPs and KDMs for a cinema's trusted devices from its FLM (#3); other camera codecs, clip-wrapped MXF and a timecode track in a rebuilt MP4 (#4); IMSC 1.1 Image profile subtitles, ADM audio track files (ST 2067-204) and several segments in IMF (#5); the 2:3:3:2, 2:2:2:4 and shifted-field PAL cadences, 25p to 50i, burn-in timecode at the delivered rate, captions exported at another rate and loudness measured after a speed change (#6); depth for reframed, 360°, stereo and nested views, and above 30 frames a second (#7); IAMF beside the picture in one MP4, Opus and AAC, and demixing parameters (#10); gain maps applied for the display's headroom, Apple's older HEIC gain map and AV1 stills under 64 pixels (#11); other languages for shot descriptions (#13); Avid media as the used stretches, a stereo source as one master clip, DNxHD's other rates, interlaced and 720p flavours and transparency (#14); 10-bit and HDR spatial video (#15). Descriptions of what moves within a shot and people named from the face library (#13) are #11 below.
- **Small Phase 7 remainders:** two takes side by side from a script line, and script revision colours (#1); true-stereo impulse responses and a room tail that rings past the clip's end (#2); Bandit v2's other languages once their checkpoints are released (#3); deep EXR and lossy HTJ2K (#9); the monitor LUT on the GPU (#11); a face mesh steadied over time and lip sync on more than the largest face (#13, #15); expressions carried through AAF and OTIO (#14).
- **Small Phase 6 remainders:** the dialogue-gated loudness range (LRA) and short-term readings; roll-up, paint-on and service-2 708 captions; Tangent controls for Curves, the Colour Warper and the HDR Palette; split screen across several stills.
- **Prompted sound isolation** and **temporally consistent inpainting** still have no model with usable terms. SAM Audio is under Meta's own SAM License, which is not GPL-compatible, and its download is gated (**flag**). MiniMax-Remover's weights are CC BY-NC 4.0, and ProPainter is non-commercial. Separating two voices that overlap (#5) covers the dialogue editor's commonest case.

## Phase 9 candidates

| # | Feature | Who has it | Size | Notes |

| 1 | Dialogue repair, the rest of the set: De-Clip for distorted recordings, De-Plosive for popped Ps and Bs, De-Wind for wind on location mics, and Breath Control to turn breaths down | iZotope RX (paid; used inside Pro Tools, Premiere and Resolve), Audition (DeClipper), Resolve's Fairlight FX (Voice Isolation, De-Esser, De-Hummer) | S–M | In-house, beside De-Click in `audio/AudioRepair`, and cached on the whole source like it. De-Clip: runs of samples at or near the clip level (found from the histogram's pile-up, so soft clipping counts too) are rebuilt by least-squares autoregressive interpolation from the sound either side, as De-Click already does, constrained to lie beyond the clip level, so the peak can only grow. A short limiter after it keeps the result under 0 dBFS. De-Plosive: a burst under about 150 Hz that rises fast inside speech (speech gating from Dialog LKFS) gets a dynamic high-pass for its 20–80 ms, leaving the voice's own low end alone. De-Wind: wind is low, gusty and, between two mics, uncorrelated. Each bin under about 600 Hz is turned down by how far its level jumps from frame to frame and how little the channels agree, with Max Reduction as Speech Enhancement has. Breath Control: breaths are found in the gaps between transcribed words (else by spectral flatness, no voicing and a level under the speech), and written as volume keys down by a set amount, so each one can be put back by hand. MCP `montage_add_effect` with each. Test: jfk.wav and a tone sweep clipped at −6 dBFS come back within 3 dB SNR of the original where the clipped peaks were, with unclipped samples unchanged. Synthetic plosives (40 ms 80 Hz bursts) drop 15 dB with the speech's spectrum above 200 Hz within 0.5 dB. Modulated low noise added to one channel of a stereo voice drops 12 dB. Breaths placed between Kokoro lines are found within 50 ms and nothing else is keyed |
| 2 | Floating-point pictures: values above 1 kept end to end, so OpenEXR, ACES and scene-linear grades no longer clip; OpenFX plugins told the clip's colour space | Resolve, Baselight and Nuke (32-bit float throughout), After Effects (32 bpc), Premiere (32-bit float effects) | L | Frames move from 16-bit integers to half floats (QImage's RGBA16FPx4, Qt 6.2 and later), the same eight bytes a pixel as today's RGBA64, so caches keep their size. OpenEXR is read straight into half floats; everything else is converted from what FFmpeg gives. The compositor, keying, blends, transforms and the colour tools (Color Correct, curves, the Colour Warper, the HDR Palette, OCIO, LUTs) work unclamped. Effects that cannot are marked and get a picture clamped for them, with a note in the Inspector. Values are clamped only where a picture leaves: the SDR viewer, integer exports and scopes' SDR scale. The GPU compositor already renders half floats for the HDR viewer, so it follows the same rule and keeps the GPU-matches-CPU tests. OpenFX 1.5's colour management (`ofxColour.h`, already vendored) gives plugins the clip's colour space by its OCIO name and takes the space they ask for, so plugins such as Dehancer and FilmConvert see log or linear as they expect. Test: an EXR holding 16.0 brought down four stops by Color Correct reads 1.0 within 1e-3, where today it reads 0.0625. An ACEScg sequence through the ACES 2.0 output renders the same on the CPU and GPU. A half-float EXR export keeps a value of 8. The test OpenFX plugin in `tests/plugins` reports a value above 1 and the colour space "ACEScg" |
| 3 | Damaged-frame repair: find frames that are broken (decoder errors, blocky digital hits, tape drop-out lines, a single flash or black frame, a frame missing from the timestamps) and rebuild each from its neighbours | Baselight v7 (damaged-frame repair), MTI DRS Nova and DIAMANT-Film (paid) | S–M | In-house. A frame is suspect when FFmpeg flags it (`decode_error_flags`, a corrupt flag) or when it is unlike both motion-compensated neighbours while they are alike. Optical flow already makes them. Which kind it is comes from where the difference lies: on 8 or 16 pixel block edges (a hit), in whole rows (drop-out), or over all of it (flash or black). A camera flash keeps its structure (normalised correlation stays high) and is left alone, and a scene cut never counts. Each frame found is listed and marked like Quality Check's results. Repair replaces the whole frame, or only the damaged blocks or rows, with one made by RIFE (already there) from the good frames either side. Several bad frames in a row are filled from the nearest good ones. It is kept as a source effect (`frame_repair`, a list of source frames) so it can be undone or changed, and offered from Quality Check. MCP `montage_find_damaged_frames`. Test: a 10 s moving clip with damage put in by FFmpeg's `noise` bitstream filter (one P-frame), a frame of random blocks, 12 drop-out rows, a black frame and a dropped timestamp. Every one is found and nothing else, including a real camera flash and two cuts. Each repaired frame is within 2 dB PSNR of the undamaged original |
| 4 | Dialogue intelligibility: a lane showing where the dialogue is hard to follow under music and effects, band by band, live or analysed, with Quality Check limits set per broadcaster | Nuendo 15 (Analyzer Track, built on Fraunhofer IDMT's measure) | S–M | In-house. Dialogue is what Dialogue-role tracks play; for a finished mix with no stems, it is the dialogue stem Bandit v2 separates (already there). Everything else is the masker. Every 400 ms the speech and masker levels are taken in third-octave bands, and their ratios give the Speech Intelligibility Index (ANSI S3.5's band-importance method, with the hearing threshold for the playback level). Silences and non-speech are skipped by the speech gate. The lane is green, amber or red from set thresholds (presets, editable). Quality Check gets a Dialogue Intelligibility entry that marks stretches under the limit for longer than a set time. The live meter shows the current figure while playing. MCP `montage_intelligibility`. Test: speech with pink noise at −10, 0, +10 and +20 dB SNR gives a figure that rises with SNR, red at −10 and green at +20. Music only below 500 Hz scores better than broadband noise at the same overall level. A 3 s stretch where the music swells in a 30 s mix is marked within 0.5 s of its start. Without Bandit, the role tracks are used |
| 5 | Separate overlapping speakers: where two people talk at once on one mic, split them onto a track each, with a third track for everything else | Premiere 26.5 (Separate Crosstalk, public beta) | M | MossFormer2 SS 16K (Alibaba, ClearerVoice-Studio; code and weights Apache-2.0, 55.7 M parameters), exported to ONNX here and hosted pinned by revision and SHA-256. **Flag:** its card says it was trained on open-source and private data, so the training data's terms are unclear. Only the overlapped stretches are separated. They are found by the Diarizer, where two speakers' turns coincide; elsewhere each speaker's sound goes to its track unchanged. The model works at 16 kHz. Its two estimates become soft masks on the 48 kHz recording's spectrum up to 8 kHz, and above that each speaker gets their share of the 4–8 kHz band frame by frame, so nothing is band-limited. Which estimate is which speaker, chunk to chunk, is decided by the speaker embeddings the Diarizer already makes. The tracks arrive linked under the clip, with Room Tone and Checkerboard Dialogue then working on them. MCP `montage_separate_speakers`. Test: jfk.wav and a Kokoro voice mixed with a 50 % overlap come apart with at least 10 dB SI-SDR improvement each, and the stretches with one voice are sample-identical to the input. CI tests the plumbing (overlap finding, assignment by embeddings, masks from oracle estimates) without the model |
| 6 | Sample-accurate sound: move, trim and slip audio clips by single samples, fades and volume keys between frames, markers and keyframes at sub-frame times, and timecode shown with samples | Premiere (Show Audio Time Units), Pro Tools and Fairlight (sample editing), Resolve 21 (sub-frame keyframes and markers; timecode in sub-frames or audio samples) | S–M | Clips keep `FrameTime` start and duration. Audio clips gain a start and end offset in samples (0 to a frame's worth), and `sourceIn` is already fractional. Picture never leaves frame boundaries. A linked clip moves by samples only once unlinked, or with its picture kept by a modifier key. Nudging by a sample is Alt+, and Alt+. on an audio selection, and slipping by samples works in the Trim panel. Markers, clip volume keys and track automation take fractional frame times. The ruler and timecode fields can show HH:MM:SS:FF plus samples, or samples alone. The mixer reads clips at their offsets, so playback and exports are exact. Interchange: AAF writes sound slots at the sample rate's edit rate, FCPXML and OTIO use rational times at 48 kHz, and an EDL rounds to frames with a warning. Test: a click clip moved 17 samples later is 17 samples later in a mixdown; a 1-sample slip; a fade starting 300 samples into a frame; AAF, FCPXML and OTIO round trips keep every offset; Undo restores the frame position |
| 7 | Audio analysis: a phase correlation meter and goniometer per channel pair, a spectrum analyser (third-octave and FFT, with peak hold), and Quality Check entries for mono compatibility, an inverted channel and DC offset | Audition (Phase Meter, Frequency Analysis), Pro Tools Ultimate (Phasescope), Nuendo and Cubase (SuperVision) | S | In-house, beside the loudness meter in `app/AudioMeterWidget`, fed by the mixer's existing meter tap. Correlation is the normalised cross-product over 300 ms. The goniometer draws mid against side as dots, and for surround the pair can be chosen. The analyser uses the FFT Spectral Repair already uses, in third-octave bands or 4096 bins, with an optional K-weighting. Quality Check: the stereo fold-down's loss against the stereo programme (dB), stretches of sustained negative correlation, a channel that is the other inverted, and a DC offset over a set level, each marked. MCP `montage_quality_check` gains the entries. Test: identical channels read +1, one inverted reads −1, independent noise reads within 0.05 of 0. A 1 kHz tone peaks in its third-octave band within 0.5 dB. Quality Check marks a 3 s stretch with the right channel inverted and reports over 20 dB of fold-down loss there, and passes a clean mix |
| 8 | Upmix: stereo to 5.1, 7.1, 5.1.4 or 7.1.4 (and first-order ambisonics), with dialogue kept in the centre, and folding back to the same stereo | NUGEN Halo Upmix and Penteo (paid plug-ins for Pro Tools, Nuendo and Resolve) | S–M | In-house, as a track or clip effect in surround and ambisonic sequences (keyframeable). Short-time spectra of the two channels are split per bin into a direct part (correlated, placed by its panning between the channels) and an ambient part (uncorrelated). The direct part is re-panned across left, centre and right (and the wides), with a Centre Width control. The ambient part goes to the surrounds and heights through decorrelating all-pass filters and a short delay, with Surround Depth and Height controls. Dialogue, from Dialogue-role clips or Bandit's dialogue stem, is held in the centre. The LFE gets the sum under 120 Hz, off by default. The fold-down Montage already makes gives back the original within 1 LU. MCP `montage_add_effect` with `upmix`. Test: a centre-panned tone is 20 dB higher in C than in L and R; a hard-left tone stays in L; independent noise lands mostly in the surrounds (their coherence under 0.2); in 7.1.4 the heights carry only the ambient part; and the stereo fold-down matches the source within 1 LU with a correlation above 0.9 |
| 9 | Open Avid bins: read Media Composer `.avb` bins directly: master clips, subclips, group and multigroup clips, sequences, locators, bin columns and custom metadata, linked to the Avid MediaFiles they name | No NLE; pyavb (MIT) and tools built on it, such as Lil' Bin Boy | S–M | Written here, following pyavb (MIT; its notice kept in `THIRD_PARTY_NOTICES.md`), which reads the bin's object table: compositions (mobs), track groups, source clips, fillers, transitions, effects by name, locators with colours and comments, and the bin's columns and view. File › Import › Avid Bin… and MCP `montage_import_avid_bin`. Test: pyavb's own sample bin (`test_file_01.avb`) gives the mob names, IDs, lengths and track layout pyavb reports. Bins written by pyavb in the test, pointing at OP-Atom media Montage made, open linked, and their sequence matches the same cut imported through AAF frame for frame. **A bin from a current Media Composer cannot be made here**; the pyavb sample stands in |
| 10 | Film counts and lists: feet and frames in timecode displays, KeyKode and ink numbers from scans and logs, burnt-in key numbers, and cut, optical, dupe and pull lists, with change lists in feet and frames | Media Composer (film projects, FilmScribe), Resolve 21.1.1 (source feet and frames in data burn), Baselight (key numbers) | S–M | In-house. KeyKode comes from the DPX film header (manufacturer, film type, prefix, count, perforation offset) and Cineon's, from ALE's KN Start and Ink Number columns and from FLEx logs, and is kept per media item like timecode. Every timecode field can show feet and frames, from zero or from the sequence's start. Burn-ins gain key number and footage fields. The cut list gives each shot's key numbers exactly, the dissolve is on the optical list and the repeat on the dupe list, 3-perf counts stay right across 64-frame cycles, and a recut's change list is in feet and frames. The burnt-in key number reads back through Montage's own OCR |
| 11 | Shot logging that sees movement: camera moves tagged per shot (static, pan, tilt, zoom, push, handheld, and changes within a shot), what happens across the shot, and the people in it by name | Media Composer (Ready to Edit, with Avid Content Core), Resolve 21 (IntelliSearch), Final Cut 12 (Visual Search), Premiere (Media Intelligence search) | M | Camera moves are in-house: the point tracker's Lucas–Kanade over a grid gives a similarity per frame. Its translation, scale and roll, smoothed, give pan, tilt, zoom or push, and roll, with speeds. The high-frequency residue gives handheld. Video depth tells a push (parallax) from a zoom (none). Each run longer than half a second becomes a keyword ("pan right, slow"), searchable and shown in a Camera column. The result fills Describe Shots' field and the search, and Phase 8 #13's single middle frame becomes the whole shot. **Flag:** SmolVLM2 was trained on public video datasets (LLaVA-Video-178K, FineVideo, ShareGPT4Video, MovieChat and others) collected from the web and partly captioned by other models, so the training data's terms vary. Test: synthetic shots made by moving a still (a pan at 20 px a frame, a 1.5× zoom, ±3 px jitter, static, a pan that stops) are tagged correctly with their speeds, and a parallax dolly is called a push. CI checks the plumbing with a tiny GGUF. With the real model, the shot names the people the panel was told and stays within the grammar |

| 13 | Particles: an emitter generator for sparks, dust, snow, rain, smoke puffs and confetti, with forces, turbulence, collisions and sprites, in 2D or in 3D with the title camera | After Effects (CC Particle World, CC Particle Systems II), Resolve and Fusion (particle tools), Motion and Final Cut (emitters), Nuke (particles) | M | In-house, as a generator clip. Emitters: a point, line, box, sphere, a mask or shape, a title's outline, or a picture's alpha or brightness. Each has a birth rate, life, speed, direction and spread. Forces: gravity, wind, drag, attractors and repellers, and curl-noise turbulence (smooth, without sinks). Collisions are with a floor plane and the frame's edges, with a bounce. Parameters are keyframeable and take expressions, so `audio(track)` drives emission. Presets cover the common looks. | 15 | Network video in and out over Open Media Transport (OMT), the open, royalty-free alternative to NDI: the Program or reference output as an OMT source with sound and timecode, and OMT sources recorded in Capture | vMix 29 and later, OBS (OMT plugin), Nimble Streamer; Premiere and Resolve offer only NDI, through Vizrt's proprietary SDK | M | The OMT project's libraries are MIT, but libomt is a C wrapper around a .NET implementation (libomtnet).
