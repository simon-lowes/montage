# Phase 14 roadmap (November 2026)

A thirteenth gap analysis, made as Phase 13 was finished and merged (main 2511cb9, 4 November), four days after the Phase 13 analysis of 31 October. **Only two releases dated after 31 October were found: Pro Tools 2026.11 (3 November) and Nuendo 15.0.40 (28 October, maintenance).** It compares Montage with Premiere 26.5.2 and After Effects 26.5 (no 26.6 or 27.0 was found; Adobe MAX is on 10–12 November, after this analysis). Adobe's beta pages for both refused this analysis's fetch (403). Searches of Adobe's beta forum found a few Premiere Beta 27.0 changes not noted before: colour labels on track headers (Build 39, about 57 days ago by Adobe's post), Audio Leveling in Essential Sound (Build 39), new audio stretching modes (Build 29), and Sync to Video, Generate Soundscape and Generate Music (Builds 22 and 30, generated media). After Effects' beta adds nothing to what Phase 13 found beyond an account menu (Build 27.0.049) and better auto-save. It covers DaVinci Resolve 21.1.2 (26 October; Newsshooter has nothing newer, and no 21.2 was found). It covers Final Cut Pro 12.4 (29 September): Apple's release notes page still has 12.4 at the top, so the Creator Studio update announced on 27 October has not shipped yet. It covers Media Composer 2026.8 (no 2026.10 was found) and Baselight 7.0.3 (build 26287, release notes dated 2 October; Baselight 8 is shown but not released). It covers Nuke 17.1 (no 17.2 found), Nuendo 15.0.40 (the forum post lists no new features; it points to the release notes) and iZotope RX 12.1.0 (15 October, still the newest). For Kdenlive it covers 26.08.2 (no 26.08.3 found) and for Shotcut 26.9.27 (its blog has nothing newer). Among open tools it covers FFmpeg 9.0.2 (18 September; ffmpeg.org's download page lists no 9.1, and a build of 1 November is still labelled 9.1-dev). It also covers OpenColorIO 2.6.0 (1 October), OpenTimelineIO 0.18.1 and OpenFX 1.5.1, each still the newest on its releases page. For AV2, the specification and the AVM reference software v1.0.0 are dated 28 May 2026, dav2d's only release is still 0.0.1, AV2's ISO BMFF binding (`av02`, `av2C`) is still a working draft, and FFmpeg still has no AV2. Five findings matter here:
- **Pro Tools 2026.11 (3 November).** Its release notes list MIDI Polyphonic Expression, an Extensions menu (with sound-library services from Pro Sound Effects, Forte, Soundly and Pibox), a Stem Separator that runs locally into up to six stems (vocals, drums, bass, guitar, keys, other), high-DPI Windows support, and takes coloured and identified across tracks to speed up comping.
- **ASIO is under GPLv3.** Steinberg made the ASIO SDK dual-licensed (its own licence, or GPL version 3) on 15 October 2025, as KVR and Libre Arts reported. No earlier analysis noted it. It opens low-latency audio on Windows to a GPL-3.0 program.
- **Track colour labels and colours in interchange.** Premiere's beta colours track headers, and OpenTimelineIO 0.18 has a Colour primitive for tracks and clips. Montage has neither.
- **IMF for broadcasters.** SMPTE RDD 59-1, IMF Application DPP (ProRes), is the ProRes application the UK DPP and NABA delivery specifications use (to UHD, HLG, audio description). Its DOI is 10.5594/SMPTE.RDD59-1.2022, in SMPTE's library like RDD 45. Its companion DPP003 (AS-11 metadata in IMF) is downloaded from the DPP after giving a work email, with some downloads for members only, so it is left out below.
- **KDMs from Extended FLMs.** SMPTE ST 430-7 (the first Facility List Message) is withdrawn. ST 430-16:2017 (Extended FLM) replaced it and is what content service providers now ask cinemas for, per Cinepedia. The FLM-x exchange protocol (ST 430-15) runs between cinemas and TDL operators with their accounts.

Every candidate was checked against `README.md`, the source in `src/` and the `PROGRESS.md` checklists with their "Not done" and "Not yet" notes. These are already there and are not proposed again.
- **From Phase 13:** OpenFX transitions, generators, matte inputs, on-screen controls and OpenGL rendering; Compare Encode; HLS and DASH packages with SCTE 35; MIDI learn; the sound-effect sampler; Canon Log 2, the VENICE gamuts, ADX, Adobe RGB, interop IDs and DNG opcodes; camera projection and lit 3D layers; long-GOP smart rendering; pinned tracks, 25 multicam angles and background calls; masters split across render nodes; JPEG XL; Displace by Layer, Painterly and Hard Mix; 9-pin deck control and LTC chase; ST 2110 receivers with ST 2022-7 and NMOS; and IMF Application ProRes.
- **From earlier phases:** everything the Phase 13 roadmap listed as already there.
- **Brought by recent releases, already in Montage:**
  - Dialogue levelled with editable volume keyframes (Premiere Beta's Audio Leveling): Auto Mix evens out dialogue within each clip with volume keyframes, up to 6 dB either way.
  - Film-mix automation with copy and paste, and punch-out in Latch (Nuendo 15): Phase 11 #2.
  - Stems split on the computer (Pro Tools' Stem Separator): Montage's four- and five-stem splits. Guitar and keys as stems of their own would need another model, and none is proposed.
  - OGraf and Lottie graphics on Linux (Resolve 21.1.2): Montage plays both on every platform.
  - Crash recovery and a safe start without plug-ins (Shotcut's automatic switch-off when start-up hangs): Montage's recovery copy, snapshots and safe mode. What Montage lacks is plug-ins kept apart from the program while it runs, #1 below.

Dolby Vision, per-bin locking, direct publishing and generated media stay out. Speaker separation and shot logging stay deferred.

Ranking is value to a professional editor, colourist, sound editor or finishing artist × feasibility in C++/Qt/FFmpeg with optional local ONNX and GGUF models. Items 1–12 can be tested completely in CI and come first; 13–15 each have one part that cannot be checked here, said in the row. Each row ends with what is out of reach. Nothing below needs a paid specification, an account, a cloud API or an API key. No new model is proposed, and no closed SDK is used. Every dependency is GPL-3.0-compatible unless **flagged**. The licences below were read from each project's repository page or its licence file there on 4 November:
- PortAudio, for #13's device layer: an MIT-style licence with non-binding requests (`LICENSE.txt`).
- PipeWire, whose JACK library #13 can use on Linux: MIT (`COPYING` and `LICENSE` on freedesktop.org's GitLab), except `libspa-alsa` (LGPL) and `libjackserver` (GPL-2), which #13 does not need.
- JACK2: GitHub shows "GPL-2.0 license" and `COPYING` is the GPL-2 text. Whether it is "or later", and the licence of `libjack` on its own, were not shown, **flag**. #13 links JACK only through PipeWire's library or a system `libjack` loaded at run time until this is settled.
- The ASIO SDK, for #13 on Windows: its `LICENSE.txt` gives Steinberg's ASIO licence or GPL version 3. It was read from a copy on GitHub (`audiosdk/asio`), not from Steinberg, whose developer page showed no download, **flag** until read from Steinberg's own download. A Windows build with ASIO is GPL-3.0-only rather than "or later".
- AVM, AV2's reference encoder and decoder, for #12: BSD-3-Clause-Clear (`LICENSE`, which grants no patent rights) with a separate Alliance for Open Media Patent License 1.0 (`PATENTS`, with defensive termination). Whether the pair suits GPL-3.0 was not settled here, **flag**.
- dav2d, for #12: VideoLAN's repository page refused this analysis (a bot check), and its GitHub path returned 404. Arch Linux packages it as BSD-2-Clause (seen in search results), **flag** until read.
- coturn, a TURN server used only by #5's tests: BSD-3-Clause (`LICENSE`). Firefox, used only as a test browser by #5, was not read again.
- Sony's `nmos-cpp` and AMWA's `nmos-testing` (both Apache-2.0, as read for Phase 12).

Everything can be built and tested in the Linux cloud container (and on macOS and Windows in the Package workflow) except where a row says otherwise. Sizes: S ≤ 2, M 2–6, L 6–12 person-weeks.

## Earlier leftovers carried forward

- **Speaker separation and shot logging (Phase 9 #5 and #11) stay out**, as deferred. No newer model was looked for.
- **Open items from the nightlies of 1 to 4 November** (the 4 November findings were fixed in 28eea86; these are still open on main 2511cb9):
  - Full-size high-quality CPU frames use bilinear chroma (`ycbcrToRgba16`) while resized frames use FFmpeg's bicubic, so a clip at 99 % and at 100 % differ. Now part of #9.
  - Tape repair is still a direct DFT per partial per hop, with no progress. Now part of #9.
  - DNG opcode lists have a work budget but still no cancel, and the DCP's XYZ conversion runs on one thread. Now part of #9.
  - Live review: wrong guesses from five or more /64s still lock newcomers out, and 8 /64s × 8 connections still fill all 64 slots.
  - Adobe RGB video is written as "unspecified" with ICC side data that probing ignores, and `HdrStill` writes no ICC.
  - OpenFX overlays: crashes during a render are not recorded, and the overlay still waits for a running GL job. #1 below removes the cause.
  - MCP `shot_changes` still writes sequence markers (`McpServer.cpp`), so they stay put when clips move.
  - `nmosNode` failed once under load on 2 November and has passed since. 7a8c08a changed how a newcomer replaces an idle connection; it needs watching.
  - Older items, unchanged: untagged 4:2:2 read co-sited with no per-clip override or migration, the S-ADM object index shift in `Imf.cpp`, FDL text copied into every item, the CI abseil fallback, client edits overwritten while following, the lupdate skip, BWE buffers and crossfade, Learned Effect sync paths, the ACES 2.0 OCIO lock, display-domain clamping, `draftSdh` loops, comp crossfades in export, Intelligibility plug-ins, FCPXML and FCP7 heads and tails, Breath Control, the sound library fingerprint, MCP `removeFolder`, `setup-deps.sh` retries and `captureWhileEditing`.

| 1 | Plug-ins kept out of the program's way: audio (VST3, CLAP, LV2, AU) and OpenFX plug-ins run in host processes of their own, so a crash or a hang bypasses one plug-in instead of ending the session | REAPER (a plug-in can be run as a separate process, per a KVR forum thread of July 2026, seen in search results), Bitwig Studio (plug-in hosting modes; not checked), Shotcut 26.9.27 (switches external plug-ins off by itself when start-up hangs, per its blog); Resolve and Premiere load plug-ins in their own process (not checked) | L | **The gap.** Montage scans plug-ins in a child process (`PluginScan`, `Ofx`), but runs them inside its own. A plug-in that crashes takes the session with it, and the recovery copy and safe mode only help afterwards. Third-party plug-ins are the leading cause of crashes in editors (the user-priorities research, `docs/research/notes-user_priorities.md`), and stability is the first reason editors give for switching. **Hosting.** Each plug-in, each maker, or all plug-ins together (a choice, as REAPER and Bitwig offer) run in a `montage-pluginhost` process. Audio moves through shared memory, one block at a time, woken by futexes or semaphores with no allocation or locks in the audio thread; parameters, automation, MIDI, sidechain inputs and latency reports travel beside it. OpenFX images move through shared memory too, and OpenGL textures through shared handles where the platform has them (DMA-BUF, IOSurface, DXGI). Plug-in windows open as their own windows from the host process (embedded on X11 and Windows where the plug-in allows it). **When it fails.** A host that crashes or misses its deadline for a set number of blocks is bypassed (silence or the plug-in's input, as the user chooses), named in a bar with Restart and Disable, and restarted with its last saved state; exports wait for it or stop with the reason. Blocklisting crashes on load stays. ARA plug-ins stay in process, as ARA shares the document. MCP `montage_plugins` gains `isolation` and reports crashes. **Test:** CLAP, VST3 (the SDK is MIT since October 2025), LV2 and OpenFX plug-ins written for the tests crash on a given call, hang, write past their buffers or leak. Playback and an export carry on, the plug-in is bypassed within the set number of blocks, and the report names it. Gain, delay and a convolution plug-in give bit-identical output in and out of process, with latency compensation unchanged. A plug-in's state survives a restart of its host. The round trip at 128 samples and 48 kHz is measured and reported on each CI machine. **Out of reach:** commercial plug-ins' behaviour in a separate process; macOS windows embedded in Montage's panels (they open as their own windows) |

OpenFX OpenGL plug-ins take and give textures on the compositor's context with no read-back. Each shader keeps the CPU renderer's arithmetic. **Test:** each effect, at three settings, renders on llvmpipe (OpenGL) and lavapipe (Vulkan) within 1/1023 of the CPU, and on Metal in the macOS job. An OpenFX OpenGL test plug-in chained with a GPU blur does no read-back (counted) and matches the CPU. Exports with GPU rendering stay within 1/1023 of CPU exports. **Out of reach:** speed on real GPUs, which only `montage-cli bench --gpu` on users' machines shows |
| 3 | Track groups and group comping: tracks edited, mixed and comped together, takes identified across tracks, and colour labels on tracks carried in interchange | Pro Tools 2026.11 (takes coloured and identified across tracks to speed up comping, per its release notes), Pro Tools and Nuendo edit and mix groups (long-standing; not checked again), Premiere Beta 27.0 Build 39 (colour labels on track headers, per Adobe's post), OpenTimelineIO 0.18 (a Colour for tracks and clips) | S–M | **The gap.** Montage has track folders with a VCA fader, and take lanes on each audio track. But it has no groups that edit or mix several tracks together, no comping of a boom and its radio mics as one, and no colour for a track. **Groups.** Tracks join a named group with switches for selection and editing (cut, trim, move, delete, fades), mute and solo, volume and pan (relative), automation writes, and record arming. A group can be suspended with a key. **Group comping.** Passes recorded together on grouped tracks share a pass id, and a lane's take number and colour are the same on every track. Sweeping a range of a take on one track gives the same range of that take to every grouped track, with crossfades at the same samples. Auditioning, rating and Best Take act on the group. **Colours.** Tracks take any label colour, drawn on the header and, optionally, on its clips. Colours go into OpenTimelineIO 0.18 exports and are read back, and AAF and FCPXML carry them where the formats have a field. MCP `montage_track` gains `group` and `color`, and `montage_comp` acts on groups. **Test:** an edit, trim, move and delete on one track of a group of three changes all three identically, and one undo reverts all. A relative volume move keeps the members' offsets. Touch on one fader writes the others' automation by the same amount. Three takes recorded on three grouped tracks share pass ids; comping take 2 from 4 s to 7 s on one switches all three at the same samples, and the mix equals a comp made track by track. Colours survive save, reopen and an OTIO round trip. **Out of reach:** nothing beyond taste |

The part's encoder (x264 and x265 with `nal-hrd`) is started at that level and told the arrival times, and buffering period and picture timing SEI are rewritten so the whole file is one conforming stream. An AV1 export copies untouched GOPs byte for byte and decodes to the expected frame count. **Out of reach:** each broadcaster's own ingest checks, beyond the standards' models |
