# Phase 13 roadmap (October 2026)

A twelfth gap analysis, made as Phase 12 was finished and merged, two days after the Phase 12 analysis of 29 October. **No release dated 29 to 31 October was found for any of the products below**, so most of what is new here came out in the days just before and was not in the Phase 12 analysis. It compares Montage with Premiere 26.5.2 (no 26.6 or 27.0 was found; Adobe MAX, where one might come, is on 10–12 November, after this analysis) and After Effects 26.5 with its beta. Adobe's beta page refused this analysis's fetch; searches found nothing newer than the page of 7 October (Contour Paint, layers as materials, Substance materials on text and shapes, a compression setting for PNG sequences and a Stock panel). It covers DaVinci Resolve 21.1.2 (Newsshooter, 26 October; no 21.2 has been announced). Among 21.1.2's changes not noted before are a project setting that turns DNG lens correction off, responsive OGraf templates, loudness meters in the edit mixer and a current-clip indicator. It covers Final Cut Pro 12.4 (29 September) and the Apple Creator Studio update Apple announced on 27 October for November. Apple's newsroom gives no version numbers. Beyond what Phase 12 recorded, the update brings Motion a new Displacement filter, lets Compressor encode Cinematic-mode video from the iPhone 18 Pro, and gives Logic Pro an assistant and a Stem Splitter that rebalances drum parts. The analysis also looks at Media Composer 2026.8 (no 2026.10 was found) and Baselight. For Baselight it found only v7 point releases (Baselight for Avid 7.0.2, 18 September; a v7 user guide dated 30 September); Baselight 8 is still due in 2027 as Phase 12 found. It covers Nuke 17.1 (no 17.2 was found) and Pro Tools 2026.4.1 (9 July; Avid's requirements page, updated 22 October, still names it as current). No Nuendo 15 update was found. iZotope RX 12.1.0 (15 October) is maintenance, with Boris FX licence activation and new branding. For Kdenlive it covers 26.08.2 (13 October on kdenlive.org, 8 October on KDE's app page) and the 26.12 call for testing of 16 October; no 26.08.3 was found. For Shotcut it covers 26.9.27; the 26.10.3 AppImage is still unannounced. Among open tools it covers FFmpeg 9.0.2 (18 September). Phoronix reported on 23 October that the "release dance" for 9.1 is under way, but no 9.1 release was found. It also covers OpenColorIO 2.6.0 (1 October; nothing newer on its releases page), OpenTimelineIO 0.18.1 (no 0.19 found) and OpenFX 1.5.1 (its release notes mention 1.6 only as future Windows ARM64 support). For AV2, a status table of October calls dav2d feature-complete for 8 and 10 bits but still being optimised, and FFmpeg still has no AV2. Five findings matter here:
- **VMAF for HDR.** Netflix released new VMAF models (v1) in June 2026 and, on 26 October, an HDR version of VMAF v1 (`hdr_vmaf_v1.1.8`), which converts both inputs to PQ ICtCp through zimg.
- **JPEG XL is back in browsers.** Hackaday reported on 28 October that Chrome, which added a Rust decoder (jxl-rs) behind a flag in Chrome 145, supports JPEG XL fully from Chrome 155; Safari has had it since 2023.
- **SCTE 35 is free.** SCTE says all its standards are free to download. The ANSI/SCTE 35 2023r1 PDF downloads without signing in (checked). The newest edition, SCTE 35-1 2023r2 (16 April 2026), is offered through SCTE's account catalogue, and whether that needs a sign-in was not settled.
- **Photon still cannot check IMF with ProRes.** Its issue #389 ("Missing support for ProRes (RDD45)") has been open with no comments since 15 October 2024.
- **MIDI Time Code and MIDI Machine Control need an account.** The MIDI Association gives its MIDI 1.0 specifications away, but only to signed-in members, so chasing MTC and MMC is left out below.

Every candidate was checked against `README.md`, the source in `src/` and the `PROGRESS.md` checklists with their "Not done" and "Not yet" notes. These are already there and are not proposed again.
- **From Phase 12:** elastic audio, surface tracking, 3D layers, shape layers, VFX shot versions, S-ADM and ADM audio in IMF, captions at shot changes and EBU-TT-D, tape repair, crosstalk, colour correlation and the grain model, clean-plate keying, ASC FDLs, segmented export, ST 2110 sending with NMOS, the GPU path and browser review over WebRTC.
- **From earlier phases:** everything the Phase 12 roadmap listed as already there.
- **Lower priority items Phase 12 listed that Montage already has:**
  - Audio waveform and spectrum generators: the Audio Visualiser generator, `render/AudioReactive`.
  - Panasonic RW2 stills: read through LibRaw (`media/CameraRaw`), so FFmpeg 9.1's RW2 decoder would add nothing.
- **Brought by recent releases, already in Montage:**
  - Round trips to an image editor (Apple's update: Pixelmator Pro; Montage has Edit Original and reloads changed media).
  - More keyframe easing (Bézier keyframes and the graph editor).
  - Clips placed anywhere on the timeline (Montage's timeline has tracks, not a primary storyline).
  - An assistant that works through tools (Logic Pro, After Effects; Montage's Assistant panel and MCP server).
  - A drums stem (Montage's four- and five-stem splits), though not a kit split into its parts.
  - Dynamic auto ducking and Paper Edit (Premiere 26.5; Montage's auto duck and paper edit).
  - Responsive OGraf templates (Resolve 21.1.2): an OGraf graphic in Montage is rendered at the sequence's size, so a template that adapts to its size adapts in Montage too.

Dolby Vision, per-bin locking, direct publishing and generated media stay out. Speaker separation and shot logging stay deferred.

Ranking is value to a professional editor, colourist, sound editor or finishing artist × feasibility in C++/Qt/FFmpeg with optional local ONNX and GGUF models. Items 1–12 can be tested completely in CI and come first; 13–15 each have one part that cannot be checked here, said in the row. Each row ends with what is out of reach. Nothing below needs a paid specification, an account, a cloud API or an API key. No new model is proposed, and no closed SDK is used. Every dependency is GPL-3.0-compatible unless **flagged**. The licences below were read from each project's repository page on 31 October:
- libvmaf, Netflix's VMAF library: a two-clause BSD licence with one added clause. Whether it suits GPL-3.0 was not settled here, **flag**, so #2 keeps VMAF optional and off by default.
- zimg, which libvmaf needs for HDR: WTFPL.
- libjxl: BSD-3-Clause.
- OpenFX, whose example plug-ins #1 builds for its tests: BSD-3-Clause.
- hls.js (Apache-2.0) and dash.js (the page says "BSD license"; its LICENSE.md was not opened): test tools only, served to headless Chrome by #3's tests and not shipped.
- Sony's `nmos-cpp` and AMWA's `nmos-testing` (both Apache-2.0, as read for Phase 12 on 29 October).

Everything can be built and tested in the Linux cloud container (and on macOS and Windows in the Package workflow) except where a row says otherwise. Sizes: S ≤ 2, M 2–6, L 6–12 person-weeks.

## Earlier leftovers carried forward

- **Speaker separation and shot logging (Phase 9 #5 and #11) stay out**, as deferred. No newer model was looked for. The camera-move tagging half of #11 needs no model and could still be done on its own.
- **Open items from the 31 October nightly** (its fixes are merged; these are what it left):
  - Tape repair still runs a DFT for each partial on every hop. It needs to be faster and to show progress.
  - Clean plates made from several frames are now cached, but they still decode the neighbouring frames for each frame. At 8K that costs a great deal of memory.
  - Shot changes detected since 31 October are clip markers on the source and follow moves, slips and trims. Shot-change markers already on a sequence still stay where they are when clips move.
  - An FDL's text is copied into every item that uses it, rather than shared.
- **Phase 12 remainders**, by item:
  - #1, elastic audio: none recorded beyond the plan's limits. Polyphonic material is not pitch-edited.
  - #2, surface tracking: the node graph's effects do not see a clip's surface track.
  - #3, 3D layers: lighting, shadows, depth of field and cards in the solve's USD export are now part of #7 below.
  - #4, shapes: keyed sizes of primitives made into contents, path points edited with handles on the monitor, an SVG group's opacity applied to the group as one, SVG dash arrays beyond the first pair, CSS and text, keyframeable gradient stops, and a GPU path.
  - #5, VFX versions: vendor manifests, versions at another rate than the plate, scripts for compositors other than Nuke, and untagged DPX and TIFF versions. Shot tracking services need accounts and stay out.
  - #6, ADM in IMF: frame-wrapped and multi-track ADM files, gzip-compressed S-ADM, programmes after the first, and object size, divergence and zones. Photon still has no ST 2067-204 checks.
  - #7, captions: shot changes inside a clip found as the check runs, and EBU-TT-D's optional metadata. The markers already on a sequence are listed above.
  - #8, tape repair: wow in speech alone, partials weighed by harmonic agreement, and azimuth for more than two channels. Real archive transfers with known speed errors are still not at hand. Speed and progress are listed above.
  - #9, colour tools: older builds do not read the new grain keys, the primaries are not dragged on the scope itself, and there is no GPU path for these effects.
  - #10, clean plates: a plate track for the Keyer inside a compositing graph, plate frames passed through the effects before the Keyer, and two Keyers with different plate tracks on one clip. The memory cost is listed above.
  - #11, FDLs: FDLs inside camera files, choosing between canvases by size, rotation and flips kept when a template frames a clip, and the framing chart. The copied text is listed above.
  - #12, segmented export: IMF and DCP masters, MXF and MPEG-TS joins, hardware-encoder segments and two-pass rate control are now #10 below.
  - #13, ST 2110: receivers, ST 2022-7, and 4:4:4, RGB and 12-bit video are now #14 below. Narrow pacing and kernel-bypass sending, IS-04 multicast DNS-SD, IS-10 authorisation and the Node on more than one interface stay open.
  - #14, the GPU path: hardware-decoded frames kept in GPU memory (CI has no hardware decoder to test the sharing), and OCIO transforms with dynamic properties on the GPU.
  - #15, browser review: NAT traversal and TURN beyond loopback, Safari and Firefox, HDR or 10-bit to browsers, and simulcast.
- **Phase 11, 10 and 9 remainders** are as the Phase 12 roadmap listed them.
- **GPU compositor** (Phase 6 #1): depth, people and object masks, Refine Edges and the HDR Palette on the GPU are still open.
- **Blackmagic RAW** (Phase 6 #2) is still blocked on the SDK's terms. **Capture** (Phase 6 #13): DeckLink, AJA and NDI input remain blocked on closed SDKs. Deck control and batch capture are now #13 below, and capture over IP is #14.
- **Camera motion data** (Phase 7 #6): GoPro GPMF, CAMM and Gyroflow logs are read. Sony, DJI, Canon, Blackmagic RAW and Insta360 data still need sample files.
- **Phase 8 remainders** are as the Phase 12 roadmap listed them.
- **Prompted sound isolation and temporally consistent inpainting** still have no model with usable terms. No newer model was looked for.
